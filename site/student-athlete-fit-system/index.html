The Student-Athlete Fit System

Score the schools you should be talking to — not just the ones you want to talk to. Eight filters, three buckets, one decision matrix. 🎯

By Keil Moore · October 31, 2026

Disclosure: HoopThink is affiliated with The Westview Group, which also does athlete-representation and NIL-related work.

Most recruiting lists start with five dream schools and a highlight tape.

That’s not a plan. That’s a wish list.

A plan starts with an honest scouting report on the athlete, a wide list, and a consistent way to grade every school on that list. That’s what this is: the same fit framework we use when we sit down with families in our own grassroots programs, stripped of sport-specific benchmarks so any athlete, in any sport, at any level, can plug in their own numbers.

Don’t trade more hours for more offers. Build leverage. Retain control. Protect development and education. Convert athletic opportunity into long-term options.

How to Use This

  1. Part 1: Complete the self-assessment with your family. (30 minutes)
  2. Part 2: Build an initial list of 40–50 schools, then cut to 30.
  3. Part 3: Score every school through the 8 filters.
  4. Part 4: Research and fill in the tracker.
  5. Part 5: When offers or real interest come, use the comparison sheet.
  6. Part 6: Use the question scripts on coach and compliance calls.
  7. Part 7: Make the final call with the decision matrix.

⏱️ Time: 2–3 hours for the first pass, then about 20 minutes a week to keep it current.

C. Your leverage statement (one sentence)

“I am a [GPA] [position] who brings [top 2 strengths] and is the best fit for programs that value [what I offer].”

Part 2🗂️ Build Your School List: The 3 Buckets

Don’t start with 5 dream schools. Start with 40–50, then filter.

BucketSchoolsWhat it means
A. Foundation / high-likelihood10–12Your film, grades and measurables are at or above the current roster average. You can play early. High coach response rate.
B. Target / competitive12–15You’re right in range. 50/50 on Year 1 playing time. You’ll have to compete and develop.
C. Reach / developmental6–8Dream level academically or athletically. Your profile is slightly below their average, but you have one elite trait they covet.

How to find them

  • 5 schools where alumni from your high school or club went
  • 5 schools that recruit your region
  • 5 schools that fit your academic tier (use GPA and test bands)
  • 5 schools that fit your financial reality (in-state public, private with aid, JUCO path)
  • 5 schools where your style fits their system (watch 2 games each)
  • 5 schools at a lower division that still pass your 8 filters

For every school on the initial list, log: school name · division / conference · bucket · why it’s on the list · coach contacted? · response.

Part 3🔍 The 8-Filter Evaluation

Score each school 1–5 on every filter. 5 = excellent fit. Total out of 40.

1. Academic fit

  • Does it have your major or academic interest?
  • What are first-year retention and the four-year graduation rate for athletes?
  • What academic support exists: study hall, tutoring, major flexibility for athletes?

2. Athletic development

  • Roster: Where do you sit against current players at your position? How many are in your class?
  • Staff stability: How long has this staff been there? What’s their development track record with players like you?
  • Training: Strength, nutrition, sports medicine, offseason plan?

3. Role & opportunity

  • What’s your honest role in Year 1 vs. Years 2–4?
  • What system do they run? Does it fit your strengths?
  • What does the depth chart look like after you commit?

4. Financial reality (not projection)

Separate these. Never combine them into one number.

  • Scholarship / tuition / aid: Guaranteed? In writing? Year-to-year?
  • Cost of attendance left for your family: $____
  • Revenue sharing: Is the school opted in or opted out? Verify it. For 2025-26, the Ivy League and the Patriot League opted out as conferences, and in the Northeast Conference only LIU opted in. An opt-out school makes no institutional revenue-sharing payments, so any NIL money there comes from third parties. Status can change every year, so confirm it with compliance. Our breakdown: Does Your School Actually Pay Athletes?
  • Third-party NIL: What is verifiable vs. “we have opportunities”? Division I athletes must report third-party deals worth $600 or more in the aggregate through NIL Go, where the College Sports Commission reviews the payor’s ties to the school, whether there’s a valid business purpose, and whether the pay is in a reasonable range.
  • Everything else: living expenses, travel home, summer school.
  • Is the aid negotiable? What triggers losing it?

5. Support & resources

  • Compliance: Who helps you review deals? What’s the disclosure process?
  • Career: Internships and an alumni network in the field you want?
  • Medical: What does insurance cover after an injury? What happens if you get hurt?

6. Culture & fit

  • Team culture: How do players treat each other when the coaches leave the room?
  • Campus: Could you see yourself there if your sport ended tomorrow?
  • Distance from home: What’s the realistic travel cost, financial and emotional?

7. Contractual & transfer reality

  • What’s in writing vs. verbal?
  • What happens on a coaching change, an injury, a transfer, a roster cut?
  • Who owns your content, who controls your rights, and what exclusivity are you giving up?

8. Long-term value

  • If you graduate from here, what doors open that didn’t exist before?
  • What asset can you build here: degree, network, audience, license, business?

School:   Total: ____ / 40
Academic __ · Development __ · Role __ · Financial __ · Support __ · Culture __ · Contractual __ · Long-term __
Dealbreaker rule: if any single filter scores a 1, stop and talk it through before going further.

Part 4📊 School Research Tracker

One row per school. Build it in a spreadsheet with these 13 columns.

  • School
  • Conference
  • Opted in to revenue sharing?
  • Head coach
  • How they recruited you
  • Roster gap at your position
  • Academic / major fit
  • Scholarship: verbal or written?
  • Estimated family cost
  • Support staff contact
  • Your score / 40
  • Next action
  • Deadline

Fill it from official roster pages, the admissions site and direct answers from coaches and compliance. Not from recruiting graphics.

Part 5⚖️ Offer Comparison Worksheet

This is where most families confuse a projection with a guarantee.

Answer every question below for each school you’re comparing, side by side.

CategoryAsk of every school
✅ Written & enforceableHow many scholarship years are guaranteed? On what conditions? What’s the cost-of-attendance remainder?
🔎 Conditional — needs verificationRevenue-share amount and conditions. Is it in writing? For any third-party deal: who is the payer, and what triggers payment, or stops it, on an injury or a transfer?
💭 Projections & introductions (not money)“Opportunities.” “Access to the collective.” Write down exactly what was said and who said it.
🔁 What you give upTime required. Rights, exclusivity and usage. Expenses and fees taken out.
⚠️ RiskWhat happens if the coach leaves? If you transfer? How do roster limits affect you?

If it’s not in writing, it’s a conversation — not compensation.

Part 6📞 Questions to Ask

Copy these into your notes before every call.

For the head or position coach

  1. Where do you see me on your depth chart in Year 1 and Year 2? Why?
  2. What does a typical week look like for someone at my position, in-season and offseason?
  3. How long have you been at the school? What happened to the last three players like me that you recruited?
  4. What don’t you know about me yet that matters for this fit?

For compliance or the athletic department

  1. Are you opted in or opted out of revenue sharing this academic year and next?
  2. What’s your disclosure and review process for a third-party deal of $600 or more? Who here helps athletes with NIL Go reporting?
  3. Are there restricted categories, or conflicts with school sponsors?
  4. Who can review a contract before I sign?

For current players (when appropriate)

  1. What did recruiting promise, and what’s the reality?
  2. What academic support actually happens?
  3. Would you choose this school again?

For your family

  1. Can we afford this if the athletic money is $0 for a year?
  2. Does this choice build more options after college, or fewer?
  3. Are we choosing a school, or choosing a projection?

Part 8🗓️ Your 30-60-90 Day Plan

Days 1–30: Clarity

  • Finish the Part 1 self-assessment with your family
  • Build your 40-school initial list
  • Email 30 coaches with 3-game film, transcript and your leverage statement
  • Track every reply in the Part 4 tracker

Days 31–60: Filter

  • Score your top 20 responders with the 8 filters
  • Verify opt-in / opt-out status for each school — don’t assume by conference
  • Schedule 5–8 calls using the Part 6 questions
  • Cut the list to 12–15

Days 61–90: Decide

  • Unofficial or official visits to your top 8
  • Get everything that matters in writing for Part 5
  • Fill in the decision matrix with your family or adviser
  • Commit to fit, not hype

🛑 Stop and get advice before you sign if…

  • A deal asks for account logins, passwords or upfront fees
  • Exclusivity covers an entire category (“all apparel”) instead of a product (“this shoe”)
  • Auto-renewal or rights extend past your enrollment
  • It uses your uniform, logo or school marks without permission
  • Compensation is “equity only” for significant work
  • There’s no written payment schedule, expense coverage or termination language
  • Someone verbally promises revenue sharing at an opt-out school

Have a qualified attorney or CPA review any agreement before you sign. This worksheet is education, not legal or financial advice.

If an athlete can’t fill out Parts 1–3 in one sitting, they aren’t ready to pick schools yet. They’re ready to research.
