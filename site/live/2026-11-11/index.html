Cavs at Knicks: Cleveland's Starting Five Is Minus-18.9

The number that decides it, per SB Nation's preview: Cleveland's starting unit of Harden, Mitchell, Watson, Mobley and Allen is a -18.9 net rating in 84 minutes together. The Cavs are -6.0 with James Harden on the floor and +16.5 with him off. That's a 22.5-point swing. Harden is shooting 37.5% inside the arc.

The scout's read: New York should load up on the Harden-Mitchell pick-and-roll and make Peyton Watson (21.6% from three) beat them from the corner. Cleveland is 5-6 and has dropped four of five. If Towns sits, Mobley's matchup with Drummond is the counter: pull him to the perimeter and attack the rim behind him.

Marquette at Michigan: 24 Assists a Night Walks Into Crisler

Keil's Take

Cleveland's problem isn't talent. It's that the film doesn't match the plan. A team that's 16.5 points better with its highest-profile guard off the floor doesn't have a shooting problem. It has a role problem. Harden is at his best as a primary creator with shooters spaced around him. Put him next to Mitchell with a non-shooting wing and two bigs, and every defense in the league knows where to send help. Eighty-four minutes is a small sample, but it's the same story the eye test has told for two weeks. The fix is a staggered rotation where Harden and Mitchell each run their own units. Until then, opponents will keep loading up on the same action and daring Cleveland to beat them a different way.

The Early Window Runs Through Nov. 18. A Verbal Is Not a Signature.

The Division I early basketball signing period opened today and closes Nov. 18. Regular signing opens April 14, 2027. The dates don't apply to four-year transfers, who follow portal-entry and communication rules instead.

Three 2027 girls' verbals to track until paper arrives: guard Sydney Savoury, the Nike EYBL MVP, chose UConn over North Carolina, Notre Dame, Texas, UCLA and USC. Business · College Sports

Tennessee's Term Sheet Is a Player Proposal, Not a CBA

Tennessee men's basketball player representatives and Athletes.org released a proposed term sheet, updated Nov. 10, after discussing it with athletics leadership. The university administration has not agreed to or endorsed it. It is not a signed or ratified agreement, and it shouldn't be called one.

What's in it: a universal athletic services agreement, a salary cap and a spending floor, a guaranteed minimum for every rostered player, a freshman pay scale, bonuses, injury protection, post-eligibility medical coverage, a 15-day portal window after the national championship, a five-year eligibility clock, anti-tampering language, independent arbitration, and agent certification with a proposed 5% fee cap.

The floor is the story. A cap says what a school may spend. A floor says what it must guarantee. The open questions are the hard ones: who bargains for the athletes, how men's and women's basketball are represented, and how third-party NIL gets separated from institutional pay.

One number to keep straight: the College Sports Commission's published estimate for the 2026-27 institutional benefits cap is about $21.3 million per school. That covers a school's whole athletics operation, not basketball alone. It is not a basketball payroll.

Keil's Take

The early signing window opens today for the boys and the girls. Watch which side gets the coverage infrastructure. A consensus top-10 center stays home in Louisiana, and the version most people will read is a local paper's write-up of a school release. That's the information gap in women's recruiting in one story. These kids have been evaluated, ranked and recruited for years, yet the public record of where they sign still depends on whether a school's SID hits send. If the women's game wants the market it keeps saying it has, the recruiting data layer has to be built like the men's: complete, timely, official. Start with every signing, every school, same day.
