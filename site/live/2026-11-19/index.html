Editorial · NCAAM · LSU

RJ Luis Jr. and LSU: A Court Order Is Not a Standard

RJ Luis Jr. was the 2025 Big East Player of the Year at St. John's. He went undrafted, was briefly under contract with Utah and Boston, and never played a game for the Celtics. Wednesday he made his LSU debut off the bench: 11 points and 9 rebounds in 15 minutes of a 79-67 win over UL Monroe. A Louisiana judge cleared him, the SEC says it won't penalize LSU, and Will Wade's answer was "He was eligible, and he played."

Maybe so. But a player who tested the pro market, signed pro paper and sat on an NBA camp roster is not a player asking for a fifth year. Those are two different categories, and the rulebook treats them as one. That's the failure. Georgia's attorney general has now written the SEC twice, asking for sanctions "up to and including expulsion." I don't need to agree with the remedy to see the problem: programs are using courtrooms to write their own rules. Write the line in advance. Sign a professional contract and you are a professional, or the path back is written down with a deadline.

Editorial · NCAAM · Players Era 8

Houston-Kansas Turns on One Leg

Tonight's final comes down to Tyran Stokes. Bill Self called him a game-time decision with a bone bruise: "It's a day-to-day deal." If he plays, Kansas has size and length that Houston hasn't seen in this event. If he doesn't, it's Houston's guards, their ball pressure and their style, against a Kansas team that has to find its own offense. That puts the game on Trey Kaufman-Renn, who scored 19 against West Virginia. Kansas needs 15 to 20 from him and something close to 10 assists, because he's the hub. Give me 16 and 8 and the Jayhawks have a real shot.

There's a bigger thread. In my view, since Houston joined the Big 12, Kelvin Sampson has been a thorn in Bill Self's legacy, and Kansas has never looked quite the same. I'm still surprised Texas never hired Sampson. That would have been a better call than Sean Miller, who is a good coach.

Editorial · NCAAM · UConn

UConn Lost, but Also Gained

No. 12 Arizona 85, No. 3 UConn 79. UConn was down 47-29 at the half, got punched early and kept coming at it. It lost on the road, but it ran good stuff and got good looks. That loss is worth more to UConn than a 30-point home win over a mid-major. I don't have data for this. It's a gut feeling from years of watching it: teams that play hard non-conference schedules handle adversity better in March, in conference tournaments, when it matters. You can't simulate that in practice. If your standard has never been raised, you can't raise it on demand.

I think of a legendary women's coach, now retired, who played only cupcakes out of conference. Her teams kept stumbling once league play started, and one top-five team once got run out of the gym by a historic margin. Then people wonder why the Elite Eight and Sweet 16 look the same every year. Credit to Dan Hurley, Arizona, Duke and Kansas for scheduling like they want to play late in March.

Keil's Take

Look at how Kansas got to tonight's final. Its freshman star sat in street clothes, and the Jayhawks came back from 11 down because Keanu Dawes and Trey Kaufman-Renn, older players who know their roles, owned the glass and the second-half possessions. Houston beat No. 1 the same way, with a guard who has been developed inside a system. Everybody builds November rosters around the headline freshman. The teams still playing on Thanksgiving week are the ones with players eight and nine who can execute the game plan when the star can't go. Recruit the star, sure. Win with your development pipeline.
