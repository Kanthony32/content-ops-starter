Game to Watch · Heat at Warriors, 10:00 PM

Curry vs. the League's Worst Perimeter Defense

Miami allows 17.1 made threes a game on 40.5% shooting, last in the NBA in both, per SI. Stephen Curry is shooting 42.9% on 10.5 attempts and has made five or more in three straight. Golden State is top five in offensive rating, rested since Monday and on a five-game win streak. The scout's read: Miami has to choose between switching Bam Adebayo onto Curry's handoffs or living with drop coverage against a 30-point scorer. Neither works for 48 minutes on a back-to-back. Watch the first two Warriors possessions after a timeout. That's where Golden State goes hunting for the switch.

Keil's Take

After Denver's win over Toronto, Nikola Jokic said that officials let post defenders get far more physical than perimeter defenders, so players stop going down there, and he thinks the low post could be mostly gone within five to ten years. That isn't nostalgia. It's a rules problem. We've legislated freedom of movement on the perimeter and left the block as a wrestling match, then wondered why every kid in the gym trains pull-ups instead of footwork. If the league wants a game with more than one way to score, it should call the post with the same standard it calls the arc. Officiating is a development signal. Kids copy what gets rewarded.

NBA · Golden State

The Warriors Stop Paying Porzingis

Golden State is withholding payments on Kristaps Porzingis' $20 million salary, contending he isn't rendering the services his contract requires, per reporting from Shams Charania and Anthony Slater relayed by Hoops Rumors and Eurohoops. The math: 1/91.6th of $20 million is $218,341 per game missed. He has been out indefinitely with an undisclosed health issue and hasn't been at the facility since early October. Per those reports, he isn't disputing the missed payments, and the Warriors are still shopping the contract, with a reported target of the Dec. 16 re-aggregation date. He's listed out (illness) for tonight against Miami.

NCAAW · Eligibility

A TRO Puts Nya Robertson Back on Tennessee's Roster

A court granted Tennessee guard Nya Robertson a temporary restraining order against the NCAA's age-based eligibility model, letting her practice and compete while the case proceeds. The order runs 10 days, through Nov. 13, with a hearing set for Nov. 16. She was expected to be available for tonight's game at Coppin State. It isn't a permanent ruling. It's one more eligibility decision made by a judge instead of the sport.

Keil's Take

Withholding pay is a legal tool in the CBA, and Golden State is using it. But look at the play call. A player with a reported health condition is off the payroll, the team is shopping the contract, and the possible endgame is a buyout. That's leverage, not a medical process. The league and the union need a defined lane for non-orthopedic conditions: independent physicians, a clear timeline, and a decision about pay that doesn't double as trade strategy. Get the evaluation right first. The cap sheet can wait.
