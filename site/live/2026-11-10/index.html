NBA · Tonight's Headliner

Celtics at Sixers: Brown and Tatum, Opponents for the First Time

Boston (8-2) visits Philadelphia (9-2) at 8 PM ET on NBC and Peacock. It's the first time Jaylen Brown and Jayson Tatum line up on opposite benches after nine seasons together. Brown went to the Sixers this summer in the deal that sent Paul George to Boston. Both men played it down. Tatum called it "another game on the schedule," and Brown said there's "nothing to talk about."

The film says otherwise. Per CBS, Brown came in at 22.3, 6.7 and 4.0 on 39.2% from three, and Tatum at 27.1, 6.0 and 7.8. That 7.8 assist number tells you how much of Boston's creation now runs through Tatum. Watch who Boston puts on Brown early, and whether the Sixers make Tatum guard him on the other end. Philly's frontcourt is thin: Ariel Hukporti is out for the season (Achilles), and Adem Bona (spine) and Dominick Barlow (back) are out too. That's why Jabari Walker has been playing center. Boston is missing only Devin Carter (illness).

NCAAM · Champions Classic · Chicago

Kansas Loses Its Lead Guard a Day Before Kentucky

No. 15 Kansas will play No. 24 Kentucky (9:30 PM ET, ESPN) without Melvin Council Jr. He broke the fifth metatarsal in his right foot in practice Monday and is expected to have surgery in the next couple of days. Bill Self said he hopes Council returns around the start of conference play in January. Losing your lead guard puts more of the creation on freshman Tyran Stokes and pushes Kansas toward bigger lineups. Kentucky's path is the three-ball.

The opener at 7 PM is No. 2 Duke vs. No. 5 Michigan State. Jeremy Fears Jr., who led the country at 9.4 assists a game last season, runs pick-and-roll against Duke's size behind Patrick Ngongba II. If the Spartans can't pull Duke's bigs away from the rim, it's a long night in the half court.

What happened: The 16th edition tips at the United Center in Chicago. No. 2 Duke meets No. 5 Michigan State at 7 p.m. ET on ESPN, followed by Kansas vs. Kentucky at 9:30 p.m. ET. Both Duke and Michigan State are 2-0.

International · EuroLeague

Report: Six Clubs in Line for Permanent EuroLeague Spots

Per La Gazzetta dello Sport, relayed Monday by Eurohoops, Valencia, Paris, Dubai, Maxima Roma, Hapoel Tel Aviv and PAOK are expected to get permanent franchises. Partizan, Crvena Zvezda, Virtus Bologna and Napoli would get three-year licenses and wait until 2030 for another shot, with priority on two more spots in the planned move to 28 teams. Nothing is official. The board is expected to see the proposal by the end of November.

Also Monday: Hapoel coach Dimitris Itoudis told Eurohoops that EuroLeague clubs should run "2" reserve teams in their domestic leagues to develop young players. And Barcelona said Kevin Punter (ankle) is out 3 to 6 weeks and Tosan Evbuomwan (oblique) about three. EuroLeague play resumes Wednesday.

Recruiting · NCAAM · NCAAW

Early Signing Opens Wednesday, and There's No NLI

The Division I basketball early signing period runs Nov. 11 through Nov. 18. There's no National Letter of Intent anymore. The NCAA eliminated it in October 2024 and replaced it with athletics aid agreements, so what seniors sign now is an aid agreement with the school. HoopThink will post official signings, boys and girls, from player, school and program announcements only.

College Sports · Business

The SEC Is Trying to Invent a Luxury Tax for College Sports

What happened: Per ESPN reporting Nov. 6, an SEC working group of ADs, presidents and general counsels is considering a luxury tax on schools that spend above the House settlement revenue-sharing cap: $21.3M for 2026-27, rising to about $22.2M in 2027-28. One floated formula is a 1% tax at $1M over and 3% at $3M over. The league wants a vote before football's early signing period, Dec. 2-4.

Why it matters: This is the clearest sign yet that college sports is becoming a capped professional league, with the same cat-and-mouse game as the NBA: cap, tax, and the question of whether the tax actually deters the richest programs or just prices them in.

Verification: Reported, not confirmed. Sourced to ESPN, with no formal SEC vote yet.

Who benefits: Mid-tier SEC schools that want cost control, and the conference office if it can redistribute tax money. Who is disadvantaged: Programs that were planning to outspend the cap through third-party NIL plus revenue-share stacking, and basketball programs that could get squeezed if football eats the tax room.

Unanswered: Does the tax apply to revenue-share only, or to revenue-share plus NIL Go-cleared deals? Who gets the tax revenue?

NCAAW · AP Top 25

Michigan Women Jump to No. 2, the Highest Ranking in Program History

What happened: After beating UConn, Michigan moved up three spots to No. 2 in the AP Top 25 on Monday. South Carolina stayed No. 1 with 32 of 33 first-place votes in a week that included a win over North Carolina. The rest of the top 10: USC (3), UConn (4), Duke (5), Texas (6), Louisville (7), Vanderbilt (8), LSU (9).

Why it matters: This is not a one-week blip. Michigan's win over UConn plus South Carolina's early gauntlet signals a more open title race than the preseason suggested, and it raises the stakes for Michigan's recruiting close this week.

Keil's Take

Wednesday is signing day, and families need to understand what has changed. The NLI is gone. What a senior signs now is an aid agreement, so the questions are different. How much aid, for how long, on what conditions, and what happens if the coach who recruited you leaves in March? AP counted 424 players in eligibility lawsuits this month, and 201 of them are playing under court orders. The rules are being written in courtrooms right now. So every program should hand recruits a standard, plain-language summary of the deal before they sign. Tell them the dollars, the years, the release terms and the coaching-change clause, all on one page. If we can scout a kid's left hand, we can show his family what's in the contract.
