NCAAM · Knoxville & Durham

No. 2 Michigan State Walks Into Rocky Top. Duke Gets Illinois.

The best college night of the young season runs through two road tests. Michigan State is 4-0 off the Champions Classic win over Duke and goes to No. 14 Tennessee at 9:00 PM on ESPN2. Jeremy Fears Jr. is running the show at 16.8 points and 10.0 assists a night. Tennessee lost all five starters from an Elite Eight team, brought in 13 new players including nine transfers, and VCU transfer Terrence Hill Jr. leads at 18.0 a game. Watch the glass: Jordan Scott (7.3 rebounds, 2.0 blocks) against DeWayne Brown II (10.0 rebounds).

At 7:00 PM, No. 10 Illinois visits No. 5 Duke, both 3-1, without forward David Mirkovic (appendectomy). Duke is allowing 57 a game. Illinois is 0-1 on the road. Read: if Illinois can't win the possession battle without Mirkovic, Cameron becomes a pin-down-and-grind night it can't survive.

NCAAM · Las Vegas · The Business

Players Era Opens in Vegas. The NIL Money Is the Point.

The eight-team Players Era bracket tips today at Michelob ULTRA Arena and runs through Thursday: Rutgers vs. No. 8 Houston (2:00 PM, ESPN), Notre Dame vs. No. 1 Florida (4:30 PM, ESPN), UNLV vs. No. 11 Kansas (9:30 PM, ESPN) and West Virginia vs. Auburn after midnight. The hook isn't the bracket. It's the money. Each school gets NIL opportunity money, and per CBS Sports the average across all 24 participants is slightly above $1 million, with Kansas reportedly getting the most. A 16-team bracket follows next week.

NBA · Cleveland

Now We Know Why Mobley Sat: Toe Sprain, Out at Least a Week

The 24 flagged Evan Mobley's absence against Chicago with no reason given. The Cavaliers have since put it in writing: an MRI confirmed a sprained right great toe, suffered Nov. 11 at New York, and he'll get 7 to 10 days of treatment before he's reevaluated. That puts four or five more games in play. Cleveland just put 68 in the paint without him, but the defensive math changes when your best help defender is in street clothes.

NBA · Front Offices

Sacramento Is 1-12 and Doesn't Want to Fire Its Coach

Per Marc Stein, the Kings would prefer not to fire Doug Christie, in part to keep rookie Darius Acuff Jr.'s first season stable. Christie is in the final guaranteed year of his deal, the Kings are 1-12 with a minus-13.0 net rating, and Tom Thibodeau and Mark Jackson are viewed as possible down-the-road candidates. Also from Stein: Indiana (5-9) is increasingly viewed as a buyer for a wing, but only if it trusts Tyrese Haliburton's hamstring; Utah is taking a measured approach to its frontcourt injuries; Oklahoma City feels no urgency to add a big with Chet Holmgren out.

NBA · Transactions

Two-Way Shuffle in Atlanta and Utah; Killian Hayes Heads to Barcelona

Atlanta waived two-way guard Keshon Gilbert, who is expected to miss at least six weeks after a non-surgical knee procedure, and signed 6-9 George Washington center Rafael Castro to a two-way deal. Castro averaged 11.8 points and 8.0 rebounds in five games for the G League's Mexico City Capitanes. Utah waived two-way Trey Alexander to open a spot for Jonas Aidoo. And former No. 7 pick Killian Hayes is signing with Barcelona, with an NBA opt-out in the deal.

Keil's Take

Players Era is the most honest event on the college calendar, and that should embarrass everybody else. It says the quiet part out loud: you come to Vegas, you get paid, about a million per school on average. That's a market price for a November neutral-site game. The reform question isn't whether the money belongs in college basketball. It's already here. The question is whether it reaches the players who generate it, on paper, with the same transparency the tournament puts on the bracket. Publish the per-player distribution. If the NCAA won't set that standard, the events will, and the schools that hide the money will lose the recruits who can read a contract.
