UConn's Shooting vs. a Defense Nobody Has Scored 60 On

No. 17 Ohio State has won its three home games by at least 34 each, holds opponents to 29.2% shooting and forces 28 turnovers a game. No one has scored 60 on the Buckeyes yet. No. 4 UConn counters with 41.7% from three, a plus-12.7 rebounding margin and Sarah Strong at 23.7 points on 54.9%.

Keil's Take

NCAAM: Start with Duke, which has one of the most expensive rosters in college basketball. Three games in, a 2-1 start capped by a 79-73 loss to Michigan State, I don't see a true star. Joaquim Boumtje Boumtje is going to be phenomenal as he gets older, and he has two years in Durham to grow into the pro prospect we all think he can be. Right now, though, John Blackwell and Dame Sarr don't look like locks for the lottery. This is the first time in a while that Duke looks very talented without an impact star.

Look at the two teams that won Tuesday night, Michigan State and Kansas. Both have star power. Michigan State has it differently: Jeremy Fears Jr. will be an NBA player, but I don't see a lottery pick. Kansas has a guy who should go top three, if not first overall. That was the difference. I think Duke has a better roster than Kansas, but Kansas has the star. At this level, you win one of two ways: you're really old, like Michigan last season, or you have a star. Duke had one the last couple of years with Cooper Flagg and Cameron Boozer. This year I don't think it does.

Kentucky, to me, is a mess. Every Kentucky fan should be looking back at the Calipari years and asking why they let him walk. The roster got older. It didn't get better. Kentucky isn't the elite-talent destination it once was, and honestly, I don't think it belongs in the Champions Classic anymore. It's not the draw it was when Calipari was there. Calipari gets the last laugh.

NCAAW: The next 72 hours are must-watch, and UConn at Ohio State kicks it off tonight. Ohio State brings elite defense and pressure. How does UConn respond? Too much of what UConn does runs through Sarah Strong right now, and that's strange with senior guards this talented. I'm not sure those guards have gotten much better. I expected KK Arnold, with all her experience, to start this season with more impact on winning, and Blanca Quiñonez hasn't impressed me either. Coming off the Michigan loss, I want to see how UConn handles another talented Big Ten team.

Then Sunday, South Carolina and USC meet in Greenville, the one game I think South Carolina might lose all season. That series is becoming what UConn-Tennessee once was, then UConn-Notre Dame: the marquee matchup of the season. Playing each other has let them take over the recruiting base as the two "it" programs. The through line is remarkable. It ran from UConn-Tennessee, with Duke-North Carolina in the mix in the Gail Goestenkors and Sylvia Hatchell days, to UConn-Notre Dame. Now, with South Carolina-UConn off the board for a couple of seasons, it's South Carolina-USC. That's a significant development, and we'll get into it later this week.

Curry's Tear Tests Golden State's System

The 24 reports what happened. Noon explains why it matters. Plus: what the Big Ten's 10 ranked teams do and don't prove, and two signings that are about role, not rank.

NBA · Golden State · Confirmed

Curry Has an Oblique Tear. The Real Loss Is Gravity.

The MRI is in: Steph Curry has an oblique abdominal tear and will be re-evaluated in two weeks, per the Warriors. He came in averaging 30.6 points, second in the NBA, and Golden State had won eight straight before Wednesday's loss in Houston.

The obvious conversation is replacing his scoring. That's not the problem. The problem is replacing his gravity. Curry's off-ball movement and relocation shooting pull two defenders, and a defense that sends two at Curry is conceding a four-on-three somewhere else. Golden State now has to manufacture those advantages collectively.

Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg is the first answer: 25 points and 10 rebounds in Houston, his seventh 20-point game. Brandin Podziemski should get more of the creation load.

HoopThink read: this is a test of Steve Kerr's offensive infrastructure. Can the Warriors generate quality shots through movement and decisions, or was the early-season offense more dependent on Curry's individual brilliance than the record suggested?

NCAAW · AP Top 25 · Conference strength

The Big Ten Has the Numbers. Does It Have the Best League?

The Nov. 9 AP poll put 10 Big Ten teams in the Top 25, tying the record the SEC set last season. The SEC has seven, including No. 1 South Carolina. Michigan jumped to No. 2, a program high, after its 77-66 win over UConn; USC is No. 3 and UConn fell to No. 4.

Here's what conventional coverage misses. Poll representation and demonstrated strength are different things. Depth matters: how many programs can recruit, develop and retain players good enough to beat elite nonconference opponents. But November rankings are a snapshot, not a tournament result. Ten ranked teams don't automatically make the Big Ten better than the SEC.

HoopThink question: are we grading conferences by their best teams, the depth of their middle tier, or their ability to advance teams in March? Those are three different measurements.

The Pipeline · NCAAW signing + NCAAM commitment

The Value of a Recruit Is the Role, Not the Rank

UConn signs Sydney Savoury (2027, signed). A 6-foot guard from Belleville (Mich.), she averaged 27.0 points, 7.0 rebounds and 3.0 assists as a junior, put up 21.4 points a game on the Nike EYBL circuit and was MVP of the first EYBL Tournament of Champions. Geno Auriemma called her a big guard who fills a need.

UCF lands Bol Kuany (2027, verbal). Johnny Dawkins adds a 7-foot-2 center from Utah Prep, who chose UCF over Virginia Tech, NC State and Texas A&M. He's the Knights' third 2027 commitment.

The scouting distinction. Savoury averaged 5.8 points for the U.S. at the U18 AmeriCup. That isn't a contradiction of her EYBL numbers. It's a different role in a different environment. At UConn the evaluation points are creating advantages against length, defending multiple perimeter spots and playing well without dominating the ball.

For Kuany, 7-foot-2 gets attention. It doesn't answer the questions that matter: Can he defend ball screens? Run the floor consistently? Rebound outside his area? Punish smaller lineups offensively?

The bigger point: recruiting rankings identify talent. They don't establish college readiness, positional fit or future production. The programs that win recruiting are the ones that know the difference.

NCAAM · Roster architecture · Orlando

UCF Is Building a Frontcourt Identity

Kuany deserves a second look beyond the height. Dawkins' 2027 group is now Kuany at center, guard Deuce McDuffie and forward Jacoby Briscoe. That's a positional foundation: frontcourt size plus perimeter talent, landed before the market fully saturates.

The question is whether the Knights translate recruiting momentum into a coherent roster or just accumulate players. Recruiting takeaway: class rankings tell us who programs signed. Roster architecture tells us whether those players can win together.

The HoopThink Editorial Position

Two Threads Running Through Today

  • Competitive identity: Golden State has to prove its offense survives without the player who creates its most important advantages.
  • Talent evaluation: the recruiting market keeps rewarding rankings, but development and roster fit decide whether those investments pay off.

The Conversation We Should Be Having

Is college basketball building teams or assembling talent?

More money, more movement and better evaluation tools than ever, and we're still arguing about fit, development and sustainable identities. The best programs won't be the ones that spend the most or sign the highest-rated classes. They'll be the ones that make the best decisions about how talent fits together. Keil's Take

A punch costs at least one game. That's the floor in the rulebook, and it's the same floor for a first offense and a 13th ejection. The league already tracks flagrant points so repeat contact escalates on a known schedule. Do the same for ejections and fighting. Publish the ladder, publish the reasoning, and announce it inside 24 hours.
