NBA · Return Night

Knueppel Is Probable at Brooklyn. Charlotte Gets Its Shooter Back.

Kon Knueppel is listed as probable for tonight at the Nets after missing the first eight games with a left hamstring strain from the offseason. Last season he became the first rookie to lead the NBA in threes: 273 makes at 42.5%, with 18.5 points a night. He finished second to former Duke teammate Cooper Flagg for Rookie of the Year.

Here's what to watch on the film: does Charlotte go right back to running him off pin-downs and staggers, or ease him in as a spot-up release valve? The first eight games told you what this offense looks like without its gravity. Tonight tells you how fast it comes back. Coby White is also probable, and is expected to be on a minutes limit.

NBA · The Business

The Dumonts Take Majority Control of the Mavericks

Patrick and Sivan Dumont bought Miriam Adelson's Mavericks shares Tuesday and now reportedly control 69% of the franchise. Mark Cuban keeps 27% and Mary Stanton 4%. The original deal lets the Dumonts buy another 20% from Cuban before the end of 2027 at the original $3.5 billion valuation, which would take them to 89%. Dumont says he intends to, "over time."

Do the math. Sportico's latest estimate has the team at $6.35 billion. On paper, that option prices 20% of the club roughly $570 million below the current estimate. It's still unclear whether the sale needs Board of Governors approval, and the arena clock keeps ticking: the American Airlines Center lease runs out in 2031, and the team holds an option on the 104-acre former Valley View Mall site.

NBA · Transactions

Omier Gets His Two-Way Shot. Dalen Terry Lands in OKC.

Timberwolves: Minnesota plans to sign Norchad Omier, the first NBA player born in Nicaragua, to a two-way deal. He averaged 18.3 points and 11.4 rebounds in the G League last season and made the All-Rookie team. Enrique Freeman is expected to be waived to open the spot.

NCAAM · NCAAW · Eligibility

Courts, Not the NCAA, Are Setting Opening-Week Rosters

AP's count: 362 NCAAM and 62 NCAAW players are in eligibility lawsuits, and 171 men and 30 women have won some form of court relief to play. That's 201 of 424, or 47.4%. A Kentucky appeals judge denied the NCAA's emergency request, keeping 30 athletes eligible, including Missouri's Mark Mitchell and LSU's Skyy Clark. The Ohio Supreme Court let stand a ruling covering about two dozen more.

It cuts the other way too. A Texas judge's stance left Arizona's RJ Godfrey sidelined, and Minnesota's Cade Tyson is waiting on an appeals court. Same rule, different zip code, different answer.

International · EuroLeague

Round 10 Opens in Madrid, and Real Are Thin

Real Madrid host Panathinaikos at 2:45 PM ET to open Round 10. Madrid are banged up: Theo Maledon sprained a knee in Round 9, Facundo Campazzo has a hamstring injury and Mikael Jantunen ruptured his plantar fascia. Panathinaikos came into the week at 7-2, tied with Olympiacos atop the table.

NBA names to track later in the round: Dwight Powell (Fenerbahce) and John Konchar (Olympiacos) are registered and could debut. Efes lost Mike James for seven to nine months with an Achilles injury.

Keil's Take

When almost half the players who sue win the right to play, you don't have an eligibility rule. You have a coin flip that depends on which courthouse you're in. Arizona plays without RJ Godfrey while 30 athletes in Kentucky are cleared. That isn't competitive balance, and it isn't fair to the kids on either side.

The fix isn't another appeal. Write one standard with the players at the table, grandfather the class of 2022 that the five-year model left out, and put it in a document that holds up in all fifty states. Until then, coaches are building rosters off court dockets, not scouting reports.
