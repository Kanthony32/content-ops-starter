The Game · WNBA Finals · Atlanta

Atlanta Says Not Yet. The WNBA Gets Its First Finals Game 7.

Down 3-2 and playing at lunchtime, the Dream put 32 on the board in the first quarter and never gave it back. Dream 80, Valkyries 69. Winner-take-all Saturday at Ballhalla.

By The Westview Group · Contributor: Keil Moore

GRAY 23 PTSHOWARD 21 PTSREESE 15 PTS · 14 REBATL 32 Q1 PTSHALF 46-26G. WILLIAMS 26 (GSV)

Two days after Golden State held Atlanta to 22 points in a half, the Dream scored 32 in the first quarter. They opened on a 19-4 run in the first 4:44, hit six threes in the quarter and led by as many as 25. The halftime number was 46-26. Series over, series not over.

Allisha Gray led with 23. Rhyne Howard added 21, and the two of them combined for eight threes, per the Valkyries' game notes. Angel Reese gave Atlanta a 15-and-14 double-double. Three players, 59 of the 80 points. That's the formula Atlanta has been missing since Game 2.

Golden State did not fold. It won the second half 43-34 and held the Dream to 10-of-30 shooting after the break. Gabby Williams scored 21 of her 26 after halftime, her sixth 20-point game of the postseason. Tiffany Hayes, who spent her first 10 seasons in Atlanta, added 15 on 6-of-10. Laeticia Amihere pulled a postseason-high 10 rebounds.

Now the league has the game it's never had. The first Game 7 in WNBA Finals history, Saturday at Chase Center, and whoever wins it lifts a trophy for the first time in franchise history. Atlanta has been to three Finals before and got swept in all three.

Scout's Note · The first 12 minutes

Golden State has built this series on length at the rim: a Finals-record 37 blocks through five games. Atlanta's answer Thursday was to stop going at the length. Six first-quarter threes pulled the Valkyries' bigs out of the paint, and once the help had to rotate, Reese ate on the glass. The tell for Game 7 is the second half: when the Dream's threes stopped falling, 10-of-30 after the break, Golden State closed the gap by 9. Whoever wins the three-point line Saturday wins the title.

Portland

Boozer, 19, Posts a Triple-Double and Spoils Ja's Homecoming

Grizzlies 124, Trail Blazers 103

Cameron Boozer went for 18 points, 12 rebounds and 12 assists in 28 minutes on 6-of-10 shooting, 4-of-5 from three. It's his first career triple-double, and it makes him the second-youngest player ever to post one, 20 days short of Josh Giddey's mark.

The game was tight into the fourth. Memphis won that quarter 33-20. Isaiah Stewart gave them 18 off the bench and Cedric Coward had 17, 7, 4 and 3 steals. Deni Avdija led Portland with 24, 9 and 7. Memphis is 5-0.

Atlanta

Okongwu Goes 29 and 12, Mitchell Misses Twice to Tie

Hawks 114, Cavaliers 111

Cleveland led by 15 in the first quarter. Atlanta answered with a 19-2 run in the second and took a 43-37 lead. Onyeka Okongwu finished with a season-high 29 and 12 boards. Nickeil Alexander-Walker added 25 and iced it at the line with 15.3 seconds left. Aaron Wiggins gave them 20 off the bench on 8-of-14.

James Harden scored a season-high 24 and Donovan Mitchell 22, but Mitchell came up empty on two looks to tie on the final possession. Same building, same day as Game 6: this was the game that pushed the Dream to a 12:30 tip.

New Orleans

Fears Runs the Show, Pelicans Get Win No. 1

Pelicans 132, Kings 120

Jeremiah Fears put up 22 points, 8 rebounds and a career-high 13 assists. Zion Williamson scored 22 on 10-of-12, every make in the paint. A 16-2 third-quarter run decided it, and the Pelicans' bench outscored Sacramento's 41-8. First win for Jamahl Mosley in New Orleans.

Rookie Darius Acuff Jr. scored 34 for Sacramento, his best as a pro. It wasn't enough against 25 Kings turnovers. Sacramento is 0-5.

Scout's Note · Boozer

The triple-double is the headline. The 28 minutes is the scouting report. He got there without a heavy load, and 12 assists in 28 minutes from a frontcourt rookie means he's making the read out of the catch instead of holding it. Add 4-of-5 from deep and defenses can't sag off him to take the pass away. That's a decision-maker, not a stat-chaser.

The Business

Front offices · eligibility · the money side of the game

NCAAM · Auburn

Auburn Will Run Its GM Job "By Committee"

Three days after firing general manager Brian Kloman over the Mantas Rubstavicius eligibility situation, Steven Pearl addressed it for the first time after the Samford exhibition. "We'll work by committee," Pearl said. "It's by committee for our staff at this point and we got to do a good job kind of navigating these next couple months." Brett Whiteside will work more closely with Pearl on communication and with the compliance department. The staff stays as is for now.

Yahoo Sports

My Take

"By committee" is the honest answer, and it tells you what the GM job really is in college right now: compliance, communication and roster paperwork. The cap-sheet strategy the title implies doesn't exist yet.

Notice where Auburn put the weight: compliance. That's the right instinct. Until eligibility for former pros and international players is written down and applied the same way everywhere, the most valuable person in a college front office is the one who reads the fine print first.

Reform Desk

An Elimination Game at 12:30 on a Thursday

Game 6 tipped at 12:30 p.m. ET because the Hawks had the building at night. The Dream won anyway, the arena held a doubleheader, and the league got its Game 7. Don't call that a plan working. Call it luck. The WNBA's championship series needs guaranteed prime dates locked into arena leases before the playoffs start. Year 30 is too late to still be negotiating for the building.
