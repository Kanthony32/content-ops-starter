The Game · Washington

Cade Drops 52. Zero Turnovers. Two Overtimes.

A career high, 11 assists and not one giveaway in 2OT. Per OptaSTATS, nobody had put up 50, 10 and zero turnovers since the league started tracking turnovers in 1977-78.

By Westview Staff

52 PTS16-25 FG3-5 3PT17-18 FT11 AST5 REB0 TO

Cade Cunningham's old career high was 46, against this same Washington team a little over a year ago. Thursday night he beat it by six. Pistons 144, Wizards 140, in double overtime, to open a four-game road trip.

The scouting read is efficiency under load. He took 25 shots and made 16 of them. He got to the line 18 times and made 17. He ran the offense for two overtimes against a team that kept answering, and he never gave the ball away. Usage at that level with zero turnovers means he's reading coverages before they arrive.

Washington had answers all night. Trae Young hit a three at the regulation buzzer to force overtime and finished with 37 points and 14 assists. The Wizards scored the first seven points of the first overtime to go up 128-121, and rookie AJ Dybantsa's midrange jumper with under a second left forced a second. Cade answered right away with a three. Anthony Davis fouled out, and Cade's baseline bucket made it 142-138 with 25.3 seconds left.

Ausar Thompson got his first career triple-double: 17 points, 11 rebounds, 11 assists, with 3 steals and 3 blocks. Daniss Jenkins added 21 before fouling out. Detroit is 8-3. Washington has lost six straight.

Scout's Note · The other side of 144

Washington put three players at 30: Young 37, Davis 30, Alex Sarr 30 on 12-of-14. Per the AP, that's the franchise's first 30-30-30 trio since the 1969 Baltimore Bullets. That's three 30-point nights and still a loss. Detroit got out in transition for 26 fast-break points to Washington's 12. Sarr going 10-of-10 in regulation should be the start of a lot of Wizards game plans.

NBA · what moved last night

Minneapolis

Wolves Win by 39. It's Their Biggest Win of the Year.

Timberwolves 133, Mavericks 94

Minnesota shot 61% in the first half, won the third quarter 36-17 and led by 44 in the fourth. Bones Hyland scored 24 off the bench on 7-of-10 (5-of-6 from three) with 8 assists. LaMelo Ball added 22 on six threes and a season-high 9 assists.

That's five straight wins and seven straight over Dallas. The Mavs went 13-of-48 from deep, and their three-game win streak is over. Cooper Flagg had 43 against Indiana last time out; Minnesota held him to 12 on 5-of-12.

76ers 123, Raptors 112

Suns 101, Hornets 96

Memphis Comes Back From 20 Down to Beat Alabama

Alabama led by 20 early in the second quarter. Memphis closed the half on a 21-5 run and held the Tide without a field goal for 8:30. NBA · League Office

Smart Gets Three Games for the Bassey Punch

The NBA suspended Houston's Marcus Smart three games Thursday for throwing a punch at Golden State's Charles Bassey on Wednesday night. It was ruled a Flagrant 2 for contact to the nose, and Smart was ejected with 21 seconds left in the third quarter. He misses tonight against Utah, Sunday at Washington and Monday at Miami, and gives up roughly $125,500 in salary.

His file: a one-game suspension in 2015, another in 2021, at least 12 other fines for on-court conduct, and a $50,000 fine in 2019 that the league tied to "repeated acts of unsportsmanlike conduct."

AP via KSAT NBA announcement

My Take

Three games for a closed fist to the face is in line with recent precedent. What I want to see is the league actually using the history it keeps writing down. The NBA has already cited "repeated acts" in a fine. If repeat offenses matter, publish the escalation: what a second suspension costs, what a third costs, per player, in writing.

Players, teams and fans should be able to read the penalty before the punch, not reverse-engineer it from press releases afterward. A public standard protects the guys on the receiving end, and it protects the league from looking like it's making it up case by case.

Reform Desk

85-23 Isn't a Game. It's a Paycheck.

Thursday in NCAAW: Duke won 85-23 at Norfolk State, and Baylor beat Prairie View A&M 121-43. That's margins of 62 and 78. Guarantee games keep budgets alive at smaller programs, and I get the economics. But they don't develop anybody on either bench. Build a real early-season scheduling framework, regional pods or tiered matchups, so the checks still clear and the games still mean something.
