The Game · Miami

Giannis Beats the Bucks. Milwaukee Is 1-14.

His first game against the franchise that drafted him: 27, 14 and 9, and a 30-point lead. The four players Miami sent north scored 49 and still lost.

By Westview Staff

27 PTS14 REB9 AST7-11 FG13-16 FT+12

Giannis Antetokounmpo saw Milwaukee across the floor for the first time on Wednesday, and he played it like a scout's report on the Bucks' weak spots. He went 7-of-11 from the field and 13-of-16 at the line. He also had 11 defensive rebounds. Heat 137, Bucks 130.

The trade sent Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis Jr. to Miami for Tyler Herro, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kel'el Ware and Kasparas Jakucionis. On the scoreboard, Miami won that deal by seven. The four former Heat players combined for 49 points. Jakucionis was the best of them with 20 on 6-of-8. Herro, the headliner, went 4-of-11 for 8.

Bam Adebayo had 21 of his 26 in the first half. Davion Mitchell went 6-of-7 from three for 24. Miami built a 30-point lead and then had to survive it. Milwaukee closed on a 30-9 run over the final 6½ minutes, and the Heat won a game they had controlled from the opening tip.

The macro read is sharper than the box score. Miami has won six straight and is 9-2 since an 0-4 start. Milwaukee has lost 11 straight. Its 1-14 record is the worst 15-game start in franchise history, and the losing streak is its longest since November 2013.

Scout's Note · Where it was won

Do the math from three. Miami went 22-of-48, which is 66 points. Milwaukee went 14-of-41, which is 42. That's a 24-point edge from deep in a seven-point game. The Bucks won the paint 56-32 and the fourth quarter 44-25, and it didn't matter. The Heat also drew 35 fouls and went 35-of-44 at the line. When you're getting beaten at the rim and beaten on the perimeter, a late run is just cosmetics.

Dallas

Flagg Gets Healthy. 33 on 15 Shots.

Mavericks 116, Warriors 94 · DAL 8-7

Over his previous three games, all losses, Cooper Flagg shot 16-of-46 while sick. On Wednesday he went 11-of-15, 3-of-5 from three and 8-of-8 at the line for 33 points and 9 assists. Dusty May's read: "He just looked like himself." Dallas led by 29 and snapped a three-game skid.

P.J. Washington added 21 on 5-of-7 from deep. Golden State has now lost four straight, three of them since Stephen Curry tore his oblique, and has scored under 100 in three straight games. Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg went 6-of-18.

The Business

Front offices · eligibility · the money side of the game

NCAAM · Arizona

A Judge Ordered RJ Godfrey Cleared. The NCAA Said No.

On Tuesday, Pima County Superior Court Judge Jeffrey Bergin issued a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction directing the Arizona Board of Regents and the NCAA to certify forward RJ Godfrey's eligibility. ABOR complied almost immediately. On Wednesday, the NCAA refused, and Godfrey sat out the UConn game.

The NCAA's position, as reported, is that Godfrey isn't eligible for relief because he's still a party in a Georgia case where it won a stay. Its letter warned Arizona that if it "permits Godfrey to compete, it does so at its own risk." His attorneys are expected to ask the judge to hold the NCAA in contempt. After the win, Arizona's players chanted "Free RJ!"

Yahoo Sports

My Take

This is what governance by litigation looks like. One court clears a player. Another case is stayed. The association tells the school to play him at its own risk. And a 20-year-old sits in street clothes for the biggest home game of November.

Nobody in that chain is accountable for the outcome, and that's the problem. Eligibility has to be a written standard with a deadline and one appeals body whose decision is final, set before the season, not argued county by county in November. Until then, every roster in the country is one injunction away from chaos.

Reform Desk

A 124-Point Game Serves Nobody

North Texas 144, Saint Francis 20. Sixty-nine turnovers. Nobody learned anything in Denton, and that includes the winners. Their staff got no film worth breaking down. Scheduling games like this pads records and metrics, and it costs the players on both benches something real. The NCAAW calendar needs a competitive floor: cap guarantee-game mismatches by a rating gap, or stop counting them toward résumés. Respect the athletes enough to schedule games worth playing.
