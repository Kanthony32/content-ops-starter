The Game · Cleveland

Shai Takes 35 Shots, Hits the One That Mattered

Down three starters, Oklahoma City handed Gilgeous-Alexander the whole game plan in Cleveland. He scored 43 and buried the go-ahead three with 32.6 seconds left.

By The Westview Group · Contributor: Keil Moore

43 PTS14-35 FG4-8 3PT11-12 FT8 AST3 REB2 STL1 BLK

Thunder 117, Cavaliers 112. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's 43 is a season high and his best night in 17 career games against Cleveland, per the AP. The shot that decided it came with 32.6 seconds left: a three to put OKC ahead for good. Ajay Mitchell iced it with two free throws at 15.4.

Context matters here. Oklahoma City has lost three starters to long-term injuries. Isaiah Hartenstein is the only Opening Night starter left next to Shai. So the game plan was simple: let the MVP carry it. Thirty-five shots is a heavy load, but the volume was the point.

The fourth quarter was a slugfest. Cleveland opened it on an 18-9 run to erase an 11-point halftime hole (59-48). The teams then tied eight times and traded the lead eight times. In a game like that, the team with the best player usually wins it at the end. That's what happened.

Cleveland got 17 and 11 assists from Donovan Mitchell, 17 from rookie Meleek Thomas in his first NBA start, and 16 from Evan Mobley. It's the Cavs' first home loss in four tries. Same two teams Wednesday in Oklahoma City.

Scout's Note · Where it was won

Free throws. OKC went 21-of-23 at the line, and Shai was 11-of-12. In a game with eight ties and eight lead changes in the fourth, that's the margin. The other tell: 18 assists on 42 field goals. That isn't the Thunder's identity. It's what a depleted roster looks like when the plan is "get Shai to his spots." Ajay Mitchell (20 on 7-of-16, 6-of-6 FT) and Hartenstein (14 boards) are holding the floor until the bodies come back.

Los Angeles

Luka Is on a Five-Game Heater

Lakers 138, Heat 117

Luka Dončić went for 42, 13 assists and 7 rebounds with six threes. He had 16 in the first quarter and 24 at the half. That's 37 or more in five straight games, 40-plus in four of his last five, and a 43.4 average over the stretch, per the AP. He's averaging 15.0 points in first quarters alone this season.

The Business

Front offices · eligibility · the money side of the game

NCAAM · Missouri

$524 Costs CJ Gunn His Season

Less than two days before Missouri's opener, the SEC ruled guard CJ Gunn ineligible. Gunn, a DePaul transfer, played three games in the Canadian Elite Basketball League in May and was paid $524. Under a new SEC rule, players who sign pro contracts after enrolling can't come back to SEC play. He has to repay the money, there's no appeal, and the ruling came too late for the October G League draft. Projected starting center Jaylen Carey is also suspended indefinitely for an undisclosed violation. Missouri opens tonight against Cleveland State.

NBA · Front Office

Milwaukee picked up 2027-28 options on Kel'el Ware and Kasparas Jakučionis. Utah exercised Ace Bailey's third-year option and declined Isaiah Collier's fourth. The Thunder will retire Russell Westbrook's No. 0 on Feb. 7, 2027, against the Knicks. Wembanyama and Tatum were named Players of the Week. Hawks rookie Kingston Flemings served a one-game suspension for elbowing Bryce McGowens; it cost him $42,234.

Hoops Rumors

My Take

The amount is $524. The penalty is a full season. That's not a proportional rule. It's a trap door.

I'm not against a line between amateur and pro. But in an era when college players earn real NIL money, banning a kid for three games of summer ball in Canada makes no sense. And a ruling that lands 48 hours before tip, with no appeal and the G League draft already done, leaves him with nowhere to play.

If conferences are going to write their own eligibility rules, they owe players three things: the standard in writing before the summer, a decision before the draft window closes, and an appeal. Do that, and you protect the competition without wrecking a player's year over a few hundred bucks.
