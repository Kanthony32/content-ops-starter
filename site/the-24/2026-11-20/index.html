The Game · Milwaukee

Dame Goes Back to Milwaukee and Drops 33. The Bucks Are 1-15.

Lillard put up a season high in the building that waived him. Portland closed on a 9-0 run. Milwaukee has now lost 12 straight, its longest skid in 30 years.

By Westview Staff

33 PTS10-18 FG5-6 2PT5-12 3PT8-9 FT6 REB+11

Damian Lillard came back to Fiserv Forum on Thursday and scored a season-high 33 in a 124-114 Portland win. Milwaukee waived him in the summer of 2025 after two seasons. The last time the Blazers came through, his Achilles kept him in street clothes. This time he got to his spots: 5-of-6 inside the arc, 8-of-9 at the line, 10-of-18 overall.

He had help. Scoot Henderson scored a season-high 27 on 8-of-12, including 5-of-6 from three. Jrue Holiday, the other former Buck on the floor, added 22, 7 assists and 4 steals and finished +33. Toumani Camara scored all 18 of his after halftime.

This was a real game for 45 minutes. There were 20 lead changes and eight ties, and Tyler Herro's driving layup cut it to 115-114 with 3:18 left. Portland scored the last nine points. Milwaukee didn't score again.

Now the numbers the Bucks don't want to hear. Per the AP and Sportradar, 1-15 is the worst 16-game start in franchise history, and 12 straight losses is the longest streak since a 15-game skid in March 1996. They did it without Kyle Kuzma and Myles Turner, both out with shoulder bruises. One night earlier they lost 137-130 in Miami in their first look at Giannis Antetokounmpo in a Heat jersey.

Scout's Note · Where it was lost

Milwaukee forced 22 Portland turnovers and scored 31 points off them. It still lost by 10. That's a shot-quality problem. Portland went 24-of-34 inside the arc (70.6%), 18-of-22 at the rim, and posted a 64.3% effective field-goal percentage. The Bucks went 18-of-26 at the line (69.2%) in a game that was decided by one late run. When your defense can't protect the paint and your offense leaves eight free throws on the floor, the steals don't save you.

The Game

NBA · what moved last night

Minneapolis

KAT Beats His Old Team. Barely.

Knicks 102, Timberwolves 99

Karl-Anthony Towns went for 22 and 12, with 17 in the first half. Jalen Brunson added 21 and 7 assists, going 4-of-5 from three. The Knicks led by 19 in the third quarter and then didn't score over the final 3:26.

They survived on a replay. Minnesota's would-be tying three was waved off for a shot-clock violation after review found LaMelo Ball's earlier jumper had only grazed the rim. Ball then missed a tying three with 9.9 seconds left. He finished 6-of-21. Jonathan Kuminga had 23 in his return. New York is 9-6 with three straight wins.

San Antonio

Wemby 32 and 11. The Spurs Bounce Right Back.

Spurs 124, Clippers 112

Two nights after Detroit ended their 12-game win streak, the Spurs went right back to work. Victor Wembanyama scored 32 on 13-of-26 with 11 boards. Stephon Castle had 21 points, 12 assists and 4 steals, with 8 turnovers. Julian Champagnie added 20.

San Antonio piled up 37 assists, 15 steals and 54 paint points and won the fourth 32-24. The Clippers hit 17 threes, with Darius Garland and Brook Lopez scoring 23 apiece, and it still wasn't enough. The Spurs are 2-0 in NBA Cup Group C.

Memphis

Memphis Never Trails. Golden State Goes 0-5.

Grizzlies 126, Warriors 98

The Grizzlies led by as many as 34 and never trailed. That's five straight wins. Jaylen Wells scored 22, Cedric Coward 19 on 5-of-9 from deep, and rookie Cameron Boozer 16 on a perfect 6-for-6. Cam Spencer handed out 11 assists. Memphis went 17-of-17 at the line.

The Warriors, without Steph Curry and Draymond Green, shot 5-of-29 from three and ran out their 12th different starting lineup. They finished the trip 0-5. Charles Bassey's 16 and 13 was the bright spot.

The Business

Ownership · valuations · the money side of the game

WNBA · Los Angeles

The Sparks Are Headed for a $750 Million Sale

Padres owners José E. Feliciano and Kwanza Jones are nearing a deal to buy the Los Angeles Sparks from Mark Walter for $750 million, first reported by Sportico, per Front Office Sports. LA Sports Report says the price covers the team and its new $150 million facility. The current record for a WNBA majority sale is the $300 million Tilman Fertitta paid for the Connecticut Sun earlier this year. The Liberty's 2025 minority deal valued that team at $450 million.

Walter reportedly asked $800 million. The opening offer was $720 million. Here's the context: the Sparks have missed the playoffs six straight seasons, the longest active drought in the league, and went 16-28 in 2026.

Front Office Sports LA Sports Report via Yahoo

My Take

A team that's missed six straight postseasons is about to sell for two and a half times the old record. Buyers aren't paying for this roster. They're paying for the league's future, the L.A. market and a building.

That's the right bet, and it raises the bar. At $750 million, "we can't afford it" stops being a believable answer on player salaries, charter travel or staffing. Valuations went up because the players made the product worth more. The next ownership group owes them a roster, and a front office, worth that price.
