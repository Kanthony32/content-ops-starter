The Game · Los Angeles & Detroit

Luka Drops 53. Reaves Drops 43 Without Him. The Lakers Are 9-2.

Friday was the superstar doing superstar things. Sunday was the tell: with Dončić in street clothes, Austin Reaves walked into Detroit and carried a road win. That's how you get to the top of the West.

By Westview Staff

DONČIĆ FRI: 53 PTS17-33 FG5 3PM5 REB5 AST·REAVES SUN: 43 PTS14-27 FG4-6 3PT11-11 FT

Start with Friday. Luka Dončić went for 53 in a 124-118 NBA Cup win over Portland: 17-of-33 from the floor, five threes, five rebounds, five assists, 20 of it in the third quarter. Per NBA.com, it's his fourth straight 40-point game and his seventh straight with at least 35, 5 and 5, tying Oscar Robertson's record. It's the 11th 50-point game of his career, and he banked a 62-footer to close the half.

Then the twist. Dončić sat Sunday in Detroit with back tightness. Reaves answered with a season-high 43 on 14-of-27, 4-of-6 from three and a perfect 11-of-11 at the line. Lakers 121, Pistons 116. Walker Kessler gave him 12 points, 16 rebounds and 3 blocks on the back line. Cade Cunningham was held to 14 on 6-of-15.

The scouting read on Reaves is all about the free throws. Eleven trips' worth of contact on the road, against a team that was 6-2 coming in, means he wasn't settling. He was getting downhill, drawing the help and finishing through it. That's a No. 1 option's diet, not a No. 2's.

Five straight wins. 9-2, tied with Philadelphia for the best record in the league. And the Lakers just showed they can win a road game without the guy averaging 40.

Scout's Note · The game plan problem

You can load up on Luka. Portland did, and he still got 33 shots up. The problem for the West is the next possession. Reaves just put up 43 on 27 shots with Luka out and the defense keyed on him. When the second option scores efficiently against your first coverage, you can't send two at either of them. Pick your poison isn't a cliché with this team. It's the scouting report.

San Francisco · Saturday

Steph 41, Seven Straight, and History From 0-3

Warriors 126, Magic 119 (OT)

Curry went 13-of-19, 5-of-10 from three and 10-of-10 at the line, and scored 19 across the fourth and overtime. With Golden State down late he tied it on a layup, hit two threes from the top of the key, and banked in a layup with 25.2 seconds left. Orlando forced OT on a putback. The Warriors won the extra period 16-9.

Per the Warriors, they're the first team since the 1950-51 Celtics to win seven straight after an 0-3 start. Curry has hit a three in 100 straight games. Franz Wagner had 30 and 11 assists for Orlando.

Ajay Mitchell Hits a New Ceiling. OKC Holds Washington to 75.

Thunder 128, Grizzlies 97 · Thunder 114, Wizards 75

Ajay Mitchell broke his own career high again Friday: 35 points on 14-of-20 in 30 minutes, and OKC beat Memphis for the 16th straight time, per the AP. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander had 30 in 29 minutes on 11-of-20. OKC is 8-2 without Jalen Williams, Cason Wallace and Chet Holmgren. On Sunday they added depth, getting Nique Clifford from Sacramento for two second-round picks.

The Business

Front offices · eligibility · the money side of the game

NCAAM · Saint Louis

A Judge Said Robbie Avila Could Play. The CSC Said No.

On Nov. 5, a St. Louis City judge granted a preliminary injunction that would let Saint Louis add Robbie Avila as a 16th roster player. The College Sports Commission refused to recognize it. The CSC says it can't enforce a state order that conflicts with the House settlement's 15-man roster limit. Avila sat Friday, and SLU beat Missouri 90-84 without him.

Context from the AP: about 200 lawsuits involving 850 plaintiffs have been filed against the NCAA's eligibility model. 362 of those plaintiffs are men's basketball players and 62 are women's players.

Yahoo Sports AP via NBC LA

NBA · Front Office

First trade of the season: Sacramento sent Nique Clifford to Oklahoma City for Utah's 2028 and Minnesota's 2030 second-rounders. OKC waived Dalen Terry. Hoops Rumors

Expansion: Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson met virtually with Adam Silver on Nov. 5. KIRO reports Seattle isn't guaranteed a team. KIRO 7

My Take

This is what happens when a sport governs itself through settlement terms and court orders instead of a rulebook. A judge says one thing, the enforcement body says another, and a 15-man roster cap from an antitrust settlement beats a state court injunction on game night.

Players don't need a judge in every county. They need one eligibility standard, written down, collectively bargained or at least agreed to, and applied the same way in St. Louis as everywhere else. Until then, roster spots get decided in courtrooms in November.

Reform Desk

Stepping Away Shouldn't Be a Trade Note

Kristaps Porziņģis said Friday he's stepping away from basketball to prioritize his health. Within hours he was being discussed as a trade or buyout candidate. The league has protocols for concussions and conditioning. It needs a clear, confidential health-leave track that protects the player and the team's cap, so a player's health decision doesn't land as a transaction.
