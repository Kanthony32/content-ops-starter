The Game · Chicago

Down 22, Wemby Goes to Work. Ten Straight.

Chicago opened 10-of-12 from deep and led 64-42. San Antonio answered with hands, length and an 18-3 run, and Victor Wembanyama went 13-of-18 two nights after dropping 40 on Phoenix.

By Westview Staff

32 PTS13-18 FG11-12 2PT2-6 3PT4-5 FT9 REB5 BLK+29

The Spurs beat the Bulls 124-119 Wednesday for their 10th straight win, closing a 4-0 road trip at 10-2. It took the biggest comeback of their season to do it. Chicago scored 41 in the first quarter and was up 64-42 midway through the second, its biggest lead of the year, per the AP.

Then the third quarter happened. San Antonio opened it on an 18-3 run, forced five Chicago turnovers in that stretch alone and won the period 34-19. The Spurs closed the fourth on an 11-2 run.

The scouting read on Wemby is simple: he refused to settle. 11-of-12 inside the arc, 8-of-8 in the paint. Chicago couldn't keep him off the rim, and he finished over the top every time he got there. Five blocks on the other end. That's 72 points in two nights.

The supporting cast did the dirty work. Dylan Harper went for 18, 10 assists and 4 steals. De'Aaron Fox added 17 and 9 assists. As a team, San Antonio had 16 steals and turned 19 Chicago turnovers into 22 points.

Scout's Note · How you shoot 57.9% and lose

Chicago went 44-of-76 from the field and 15-of-29 from three. That's a 70.9% true-shooting night. They still lost, because San Antonio took 98 shots to their 76. That 22-shot gap came from 19 Bulls turnovers against 6 for the Spurs, plus 11 Spurs offensive rebounds. Chicago won the efficiency battle and lost the possession game. That's the hardest lesson in basketball, and the Bulls are 1-9 learning it.

The Game

NBA · what moved last night

Portland

Portland Sweeps the Champs in 48 Hours

Trail Blazers 118, Thunder 108

Two nights, two wins over Oklahoma City: 123-103 on Tuesday, 118-108 on Wednesday. Deni Avdija went for 28, and he and Damian Lillard combined for 20 of Portland's 30 fourth-quarter points. Jrue Holiday gave them 19 off the bench on 5-of-8 from three.

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander scored 31, with 23 after halftime, per the AP, and OKC took the lead late. Then Avdija's free throws put Portland back on top 99-98 with 5:24 left, and the Blazers never gave it back. Portland went 25-of-26 at the line. The Thunder are 8-4 and have lost two straight.

Houston

Houston Ends the Streak. Curry Leaves for an MRI.

Rockets 115, Warriors 108

Golden State's eight-game win streak ended in Houston, and that was the least of the Warriors' problems. Steph Curry hit a first-quarter three, landed in pain and went straight to the locker room. He played 5:35 and left the arena for an MRI on his abdomen.

Alperen Sengun ran the offense: 19 points, 13 rebounds, 9 assists. Kevin Durant added 20, Amen Thompson 19 with 15 in the third, and Reed Sheppard hit two threes in an 8-0 run after Golden State got within three with nine minutes left. Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg kept Golden State close with 25 and 10.

Milwaukee

Lakers Make It 7 Straight. Bucks Make It 8.

Lakers 123, Bucks 112

Luka Doncic scored 32 on 10-of-25, with five threes. Sandro Mamukelashvili matched his season high for the second time in three days with 23 on 9-of-15. Austin Reaves added 18. The Lakers are 11-2, the best record in the league.

The real story is on the glass. L.A. grabbed 19 offensive rebounds for 33 second-chance points, and Jake LaRavia pulled seven offensive boards without scoring. Milwaukee is 1-11. Tyler Herro had 27, and he and Ziaire Williams picked up technicals after a late third-quarter scuffle.

NCAAM · NCAAW · Signing Day

The Early Signing Period Opened Wednesday

Programs across the country put 2027 classes in ink on Wednesday. Purdue landed a guard from Georgia and an in-state 7-footer. FGCU announced its signings. West Virginia and Missouri closed on women's classes that reach into Canada, Iowa, Pennsylvania, New Jersey and the U.K. Most of these kids won't take a college possession until next November, and their schools are already planning for them.

FGCU Athletics WVU Athletics

My Take

The portal turned rosters into one-year leases. That's exactly why November signees matter more now, not less. A freshman you sign early is the one roster spot you control for more than 12 months.

The programs that win the next five years will be the ones that treat high school recruiting as their development pipeline and use the portal to fill holes, not the other way around. Evaluate early, develop on purpose and keep your own kids. Everything else is renting.

Houston · Third quarter

Marcus Smart Throws a Punch, Gets Tossed

Late in the third, Marcus Smart and Warriors center Charles Bassey went face to face during a dead ball. Smart threw a right hand that appeared to graze Bassey's nose. He was ejected, and he was already walking to the tunnel. Ime Udoka said Smart was upset that Bassey wrapped up his elbow on the other end. It's going to be all over your timeline, and the league office has a decision to make.

Reform Desk

Four One-Win Teams on Nov. 12

Milwaukee 1-11. Utah 1-11, losers of 10 straight. Sacramento 1-10. Chicago 1-9, losers of eight. That's four teams with one win each before Thanksgiving. Some of that is injuries and youth. But when 13% of the league is this far gone in the third week, the lottery is still paying more for losing than the league wants to admit. Flatten the odds further, or tie them to more than one season's record.
