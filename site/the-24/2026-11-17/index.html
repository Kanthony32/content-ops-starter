The Game · Brooklyn

Embiid Drops 37. Philly Takes the East.

A game-time decision on the back end of a back-to-back put up a season high, Jaylen Brown bled for 31 and 13, and the Sixers went to 4-0 without LeBron James. Boston lost. Philadelphia sits alone at the top of the East.

By Westview Staff

37 PTS13-22 FG2-4 3PT9-10 FT24 1ST-HALF PTS8 FOULS DRAWN

Joel Embiid sat out Sunday's win over Indiana. Per the AP, the Sixers didn't list him as available until about 30 minutes before tip in Brooklyn. Then he put 13 on the Nets in the first quarter, 24 by halftime and a season-high 37 by the end. Philadelphia won 135-123 and moved to 12-3. Boston is 11-3. That's first place in the East.

The scouting read: Embiid hunted the nail and the elbows and made Brooklyn foul him. He went 13-of-22 and 9-of-10 at the line, and drew eight fouls. When the Nets came back from 19 down to make it 106-101 early in the fourth, he scored six straight Philadelphia points to push it back to double digits. When they closed to four late, he buried a three with 51 seconds left. Game over.

Jaylen Brown matched his season high with 31 and pulled 13 boards. A fourth-quarter collision with Julius Randle opened a cut on his head; he came back in a headband and left again, per the AP. Tyrese Maxey ran it: 24 points, 9 assists, 4-of-8 from deep, 6-of-6 at the line. LeBron James sat the second half of the back-to-back, one night after passing Jason Kidd for third on the all-time assists list. Philly is 4-0 without him.

Brooklyn's offense was real. Randle scored 33 on 13-of-20, Michael Porter Jr. added 25, and second-year guard Nolan Traore matched his career high with 23. The Nets are 3-10 anyway. Next for Philly: Wednesday in Orlando, against a Magic team that just won its fourth straight.

Scout's Note · Where it was won

Check the stripe. Philadelphia went 32-of-38 (84.2%). Brooklyn went 22-of-34 (64.7%). That's 10 more made free throws in a 12-point game. Add a 22-12 edge in second-chance points off 14 Sixer offensive boards and you have the margin. Brooklyn shot 50% from the floor and 41.9% from three. It still lost by 12, because it gave away possessions at the rim and points at the line.

Cleveland

Spida Goes 16-of-25 for 39. Cleveland Owns the Paint.

Cavaliers 126, Bulls 115

Donovan Mitchell answered every Chicago push: 39 points on 16-of-25, 14-of-20 inside the arc, 5-of-5 at the line, three steals. Twenty-two of his points came in the paint. Cleveland scored 68 there as a team and won the glass 51-37, without Evan Mobley.

James Harden handed out 10 assists. Jarrett Allen went for 13 and 14. Norman Powell led Chicago with 27. The Cavs are 8-7; the Bulls are 3-10.

Timberwolves 106, Trail Blazers 98

Boston

Orlando Wins the Fourth 40-28 and Ends Boston's Run

Magic 115, Celtics 105

Down 77-75 after three, Orlando closed with a 40-point quarter. Desmond Bane scored 24 with 8 boards. Franz Wagner went 21-8-9, and Anthony Black added 20 and 7 assists. The Magic have won four straight; Boston's six-game streak is over.

Scout's Note · Boozer

Seven threes on 13 attempts from a rookie forward changes how you guard Memphis. Drop the big and he pops. Switch and Edey seals. The 37.5% on twos (3-of-8) is the development piece: finishing through length is the next step. Seven assists says he's already reading the second defender.

Scout's Note · Dybantsa

Twenty-one points, matching his season high, on a night his team lost by 16. Twelve of them came in the paint and he got to the line eight times. The jumper (1-of-7 from deep) is the summer project. The downhill game is already NBA-ready.

The Business

Front offices · trade requests · the money side of the game

NBA · New Orleans

Dejounte Murray Asks Out. The Season's First Trade Request.

Dejounte Murray's agent, Rich Paul, told New Orleans that Murray wants to be traded, and the Pelicans confirmed it, per Yahoo Sports. It's the first trade request of the season. The expected return is small, and the deal could drag to the February deadline. The same night, New Orleans snapped a six-game skid behind Jeremiah Fears' first triple-double and sits at 5-10.

Elsewhere on the ledger: Matas Buzelis (Bulls) and Josh Minott (Nets) were each fined $25,000 for obscene gestures toward the stands. Sacramento signed veteran center Mason Plumlee. The 2028 All-Star Game goes to Miami, its first since 1990. In Washington, eight straight losses have put coach Brian Keefe's future in question, per the AP.

Yahoo Sports AP on Washington

My Take

A November trade request is a leverage decision, and it rarely goes the player's way. Everybody in all 29 other front offices now knows New Orleans has to move him. That's how a small return gets smaller.

The real read is roster direction. The Pelicans' 2025 No. 7 pick just put up 19-10-10 and ran the offense in a 21-point win. When the young guard is the one making the reads, the veteran guard becomes a trade chip. Build around Fears, take the picks, and stop paying for the middle.

The Culture

Denver · Milestone

1,000 Assists, Each Way

Per the AP, Jamal Murray and Nikola Jokic became the first teammates to record 1,000 or more assists to each other since tracking began in 1997-98. David Adelman called it "a mind-blowing stat." That's two-man game chemistry you can measure.

Miami · Chasing History

Klay Is 21 Threes From Ray Allen

Klay Thompson hit five threes against Houston, reaching 2,952 for his career, 21 behind Ray Allen (2,973) for third all-time. On the other bench, Kevin Durant's 22 left him 83 points shy of 33,000. Only LeBron, Kareem, Karl Malone and Kobe have gotten there.

AP via WSVN

Reform Desk

Fines Go One Direction

Two players, $25,000 each, for gestures toward the stands. Fair. Players have to hold the line. But the public accountability only runs one way. Publish the fan-ejection numbers every month, arena by arena. If you're going to fine the reaction, show us you're policing the provocation.
