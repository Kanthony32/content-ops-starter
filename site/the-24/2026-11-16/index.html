The Game · Phoenix

Luka Drops 43. The West Now Has Two 12-2 Teams.

No Reaves, a sore ankle, a 31-point lead nearly handed back. The Lakers won their eighth straight anyway, and San Antonio answered with its twelfth.

By Westview Staff

43 PTS13-28 FG5 3PM12-14 FT25 1ST-HALF PTS

Luka Doncic went for 43 in Phoenix on Sunday and the Lakers beat the Suns 131-123. That is eight straight wins and a 12-2 record. He did it without Austin Reaves, who sat with a bruised heel, and four days after rolling an ankle in that same Wednesday game against Milwaukee. He showed no limitations.

The math tells you how he got there. Thirteen field goals, five of them threes, and 12-of-14 at the line. He had 25 at the half and Los Angeles led 64-54. Then the Lakers won the third quarter 40-26 and were up 31 late in the period. Quentin Grimes added 22 on 5-of-6 from deep, including the three with 34 seconds left that ended the argument.

The argument should never have started. Phoenix scored 43 in the fourth quarter. Collin Gillespie had 26, twelve of them in the fourth, and cut it to 126-121 on a driving layup with 1:05 left. Devin Booker went 4-of-14, 0-of-4 from three, and did not play in the fourth. The Suns' bench put up 78. That is not a comeback built on stars. That is a starting unit getting comfortable.

Context matters here. San Antonio is also 12-2 and has won twelve straight. The Lakers lead the league in scoring. Two West teams are tied at the top in mid-November, and one of them is doing it with its second-best creator in street clothes.

Scout's Note · The fourth quarter

Phoenix went 22-of-26 at the rim and turned 15 Lakers turnovers into 25 points. The Lakers won the first three quarters by 24 and lost the fourth 43-27. That is the tape I want from this game: what changed in the Lakers' coverage at the rim and their ball security once the lead hit 31. Good teams close with the same tempo that built the lead.

NCAAW · Greenville, S.C. · Sunday

South Carolina Shared It. USC Didn't. No. 1 Beats No. 3, 89-85.

By Westview Staff

T. JOHNSON 28 (CAREER HIGH)EDWARDS 20 PTS · 10 REBKITTS 15-12SCAR 55% FGUSC 42% FG

No. 1 South Carolina beat No. 3 USC 89-85 at Bon Secours Wellness Arena in the fight for "The Real SC." Tessa Johnson scored a career-high 28, with 17 in the first half on 6-of-7 shooting and 3-of-3 from three. Joyce Edwards had 20 and 10. Chloe Kitts added 15 and 12 for her third straight double-double. South Carolina is 4-0.

USC opened up 13-5 and led most of the first half. South Carolina closed the half on a 9-0 run to go up 49-45 and never trailed again. The Gamecocks led by 12 with under four minutes left. Threes from Jazzy Davidson and JuJu Watkins cut it to two with 42 seconds to go. Jordan Lee blocked Watkins on a fast-break drive, Dawn Staley's challenge overturned the call on the floor, and Johnson iced it at the line with 13 seconds left.

Watkins scored 25 on 8-of-12. Saniyah Hall had 17 and nine. Kennedy Smith had 15. USC had scored 100-plus in each of its previous three games and is now 3-1.

My Take · Who, what, when, why

USC has three of the best players in the country on its roster in JuJu, Jazzy and Hall. Now they have to figure out how to move the ball and share it. Way too many one-pass and zero-pass possessions.

This team lacks a floor general who knows the who, what, when and why: who gets the ball, when to get it to them, why they get it, and where they get it. Talent creates shots. A point guard decides which shot the team needs.

Here is the development that matters. Neither team started a traditional point guard. South Carolina's kids shared the ball and played together. Southern Cal's kids struggled to move it, to get it to the second side, and to make the defense work. Same structure, different habits, and the habits decided a four-point game.

The Business

Roster economics · front offices · the money side of the game

NCAAM · Roster Economics

A $20M Roster Needed a Buzzer-Beater Against an 0-4 Team

CBS Sports' October audit put the average high-major men's roster at $13.5 million for 2026-27, nearly double last year's $7 million. Kentucky sits in the top tier at $20 million or more. Virginia sits in the $12.1M-$15M tier. This weekend, Kentucky beat winless Northern Arizona by one on a late three and is 3-1. Virginia came from double digits down against Maryland, a team in its own tier, and is 5-0.

NBA · Front Office

The Nov. 12 Line Has Passed

Free agents who sign a standard deal from Nov. 12 on cannot be traded this season, because the three-month rule runs past the Feb. 11 deadline. Two-way signings only carry a 30-day restriction. Translation for front offices: any veteran you add now is a rental through April, not a trade chip.

Hoops Rumors

My Take

Money buys the talent. It does not buy the fit, the reps, or the defensive communication that wins a one-possession game in November. Every program in the top tier can say it paid for a contender. Fewer can say they built one.

Here is what I want from the industry: publish the spend next to the outcomes, every year, in one place. Schools are paying players now. Boosters, donors and families deserve to see what that money is producing. Transparency is how you keep this system from becoming an auction with no accountability.

Reform Desk

Flagrants Should Remember

Draymond Green was called for a flagrant on Victor Wembanyama Friday, and Wembanyama said afterward he was not surprised. That reaction is the problem. When the targeted player expects it, the deterrent is not working. Flagrant penalties should carry a player's history, not reset as if every contact is a first offense.

The HoopThink Editorial Position

Four broader developments from this weekend's conversations
1

Systems matter more than early-season rankings. The best teams are not necessarily those with the most recognizable names. They are those whose personnel can execute a coherent identity against different styles.

2

Roster construction is becoming a competitive discipline. Baylor's early success is an example of how a new group can establish identity through collective behaviors. Recruiting rankings alone cannot tell us whether a roster fits together.

3

Player development must remain part of the recruiting equation. Acquiring talent is one responsibility. Improving that talent is another. The programs that consistently connect the two will have a meaningful advantage.

4

Grassroots basketball needs measurable accountability. Exposure, evaluation and relationships have value. But families should be able to understand what they are receiving and whether it is producing meaningful opportunities.

The Conversation

Are we confusing access with opportunity in basketball?

A player can attend the biggest tournaments, receive rankings, accumulate followers and be photographed with college coaches.

None of those things automatically creates a college basketball opportunity.

Likewise, a program can assemble a highly ranked recruiting class without developing the players it acquires.

We need to distinguish between

Visibility and evaluation.
Recruiting interest and actual opportunity.
Talent acquisition and player development.
Roster construction and team building.
Winning games and building sustainable programs.

The question for coaches, parents, scouts and program directors: Are we building an ecosystem that makes players better and connects them with the right opportunities, or have we become too comfortable measuring the activity surrounding basketball instead of the actual development of the athlete?

That's a conversation worth having.
