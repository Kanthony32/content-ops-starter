The Game · NCAAM · Durham, N.C.

Stojakovic Drops 46 in Cameron. No Visitor Ever Scored More.

No. 10 Illinois went into the toughest building in the sport, shot 18.8% from three, and still won by 21. That's what happens when one guy decides the paint and the line belong to him.

By Westview Staff

46 PTS14 FGM2 3PM16 FTMOpponent record at Cameron

Illinois 99, Duke 78. Read that score again, then read the shooting splits. The Illini hit 43.1% from the field and 18.8% from deep. On most nights in Durham that's a 15-point loss. Andrej Stojakovic turned it into a 21-point win, and per Illinois he set the opponent scoring record at Cameron Indoor Stadium doing it.

The scouting read: he didn't need the three ball. Two made threes, 14 field goals, 16 free throws. That's a downhill scorer attacking closeouts and getting to his spots off two feet. When the jumper isn't falling, you go to the rim and you go to the line. He did both, all night.

He had help. Quentin Coleman scored 27, 13 of them at the free-throw line. Stojakovic and Coleman combined for 73 of Illinois' 99. Illinois led 42-32 at the half and won the second half 57-46.

Duke got 17 from Joaquim Boumtje Boumtje and 15 from Caleb Foster, and Deron Rippey Jr. handed out 7 assists. It wasn't close to enough. The Blue Devils fall to 3-2. Illinois is 4-1, and the only loss is at Texas Tech.

Scout's Note · Where it was won

Glass and the stripe. Illinois out-rebounded Duke 54-34, a plus-20 margin, and shot 79.5% at the line to Duke's 63.6%. Duke turned it over 14 times to Illinois' 13, so this wasn't a turnover game. It was a possession game: Illinois owned the boards and cashed the free throws Duke left on the floor.

The Game

NBA · what moved last night

San Antonio

Detroit Ends the Spurs' 12-Game Run

Pistons 105, Spurs 93

San Antonio hadn't lost since Oct. 23. Down 14 in the second quarter, Detroit answered with a 21-7 run and never looked back after taking a 55-54 lead into the half. Cade Cunningham scored 27. Daniss Jenkins added 25, 21 of them after halftime. Ausar Thompson filled every column: 14 points, 9 rebounds, 12 assists, 5 steals.

The tell: Detroit won the middle two quarters 52-39 and forced 15 turnovers, turning them into 22 points. Victor Wembanyama gave the Spurs 25 and 18, but went 1-of-8 from three, and the Spurs shot 9-of-40 (22.5%) from deep as a team.

Inglewood

Sensabaugh Drops 25 in the Fourth. Utah's Skid Is Over.

Jazz 132, Clippers 107

The game was 88-86 going to the fourth. Then Brice Sensabaugh went 10-of-11 in the quarter, 5-of-6 from three, and scored 25 of his 40 off the bench. He finished 8-of-11 from deep with 11 boards. Utah won the fourth 44-21 on 18-of-22 shooting.

Keyonte George ran the show with 30 and 11. It's Utah's first win since Oct. 23 and its first on the road all season. The Jazz are 2-13, but this was proof the shot-making is real.

Toronto

Brunson Closes Again. Fourteen Straight Over Toronto.

Knicks 111, Raptors 102

Second night in a row in Toronto, second straight dagger from Jalen Brunson. He scored 31 on 10-of-22 and went 7-for-7 at the line, and his late four-point play put it away. New York won the fourth 31-19.

Karl-Anthony Towns posted 16 points, 16 rebounds and 8 assists on 7-of-9. That's 14 straight double-doubles to open the season, a franchise record. The Knicks are 8-6 and have beaten Toronto 14 straight times. Ja'Kobe Walter hit seven threes for 25 in the loss.

NCAAW · Honors

Hidalgo Does Something No D-I Player Has Done

The USBWA named Notre Dame's Hannah Hidalgo one of its national players of the week. Against DePaul she went for 31, 7 and 7 and reached 500 career steals, making her the first Division I player, men or women, with 2,500 points, 500 rebounds, 500 assists and 500 steals. Louisville's Imari Berry, Iowa's Ava Heiden, South Carolina's Tessa Johnson and Nebraska's Britt Prince were also honored. The Business

Front offices · college finance · the money side of the game

NCAAM · NCAAW · College athletics finance

Former Iowa State AD Calls the NIL System "Money Laundering"

Jamie Pollard ran Iowa State athletics for 21 years before stepping down this fall. At a seminar Tuesday, per AP, he said third-party NIL deals let Power Four schools blow past the House settlement's $21.5 million revenue-sharing cap, with some donations steered into school-affiliated businesses that broker the deals. He warned the lack of transparency could lead to "the next savings and loans crisis in higher education."

The numbers he and the panel put on the table: a GAO report found 94% of Division I programs spent more than they generated in 2023-24. Towson AD Steve Eigenbrot compared the roughly $45,000 average G League salary to six- and seven-figure college NIL payments.

AP via Yahoo Sports

My Take

A cap nobody can verify isn't a cap. It's a suggestion. Schools report contracts to the College Sports Commission, but there's almost no verified public information on what each program actually spends. That's how you get distrust in every conference office.

The fix is disclosure. Publish roster spend by school, the same way pro leagues publish payrolls. Families and recruits deserve to see the market they're negotiating in, and athletic directors deserve to compete on the same set of rules.

Reform Desk

The G League Is Getting Outbid by Sophomores

If the average G League salary is about $45,000 and a rotation college player can clear six figures in NIL, the development pipeline has flipped. The NBA built its minor league to be the alternative to college. Right now it can't compete for the same player. Raise the G League floor, or accept that college is the NBA's real farm system and fund it like one.
