NCAAW · UConn · After the Loss

Geno Turns on His Own Team, and on His Own Schedule

The story out of Uncasville wasn't the 65-56 score. It was what Geno Auriemma said when it was over: layups, fouls, maturity, and whether he set this team up to fail.

By Westview Staff

UCONN 4-326.6 FG%22.2 3PT%18 TO48-40 REBSTRONG 22 PTS · 12 REB

Per the AP, Auriemma opened with the numbers he doesn't like. "Never had a team at UConn miss as many layups as this team misses or commit as many stupid fouls as this team does," he said. And: "Hard to beat the best team in the country when you're beating yourself. Incredibly frustrating."

Then he questioned his own coaching and the calendar. "I'm having a really hard time coaching this team," he said, and added, "It would be nice if I could get a little bit of help." He called the Huskies "really immature" and said it's "a very, very, very immature team basketball-wise." On the schedule: "These are basically tests to see whether or not you're on your way to being prepared... That was the whole point of putting the schedule together, but maybe I did this team a disservice." And: "Maybe they're just not mentally ready for something like this. Maybe it's just too much for them."

The record backs up the mood. UConn is 4-3, and the AP notes it's only the third time in 40 years the program has started that poorly (1986-87 and 2023-24 are the others). All three losses came against top-three teams: No. 1 South Carolina, No. 2 Michigan and No. 3 Louisville. Michigan was a 77-66 loss in Ann Arbor on Nov. 5, when Sarah Strong had 13 points and three first-quarter fouls.

Strong, the reigning AP Player of the Year, had 22 points and 12 rebounds against South Carolina but again spent time on the bench in foul trouble. Her read, per the AP: "I think it has to be a different intensity about us." And: "I'm tired of having the same conversation, but something has to change and has to start with us." UConn gets No. 5 Duke on Saturday at TD Garden in Boston.

Scout's Note · Where it was decided

One quarter. South Carolina won the second 27-6 on a run that, per the AP, went 22-2 before halftime, and led 38-22 at the break. UConn won the other three quarters 50-38 (16-11, 13-9, 21-18) and outscored the Gamecocks 34-27 after the half. It did that shooting 26.6% with 18 turnovers, without KK Arnold and Kayleigh Heckel. That's a team that can play with the best team in the country for 30 minutes and doesn't yet know how to stop a run.

The Take · Opinion

Geno Is Raw. UConn Is Still Good. Here's the Fix.

I read this as a coach who's a little raw after a loss. Look at the three. At Michigan, UConn's best player did not play well. Louisville played about as well as a team can. And South Carolina is a team UConn was supposed to lose to on paper, and I thought the Huskies played really well against them for long stretches. The second quarter (27-6) was the game. They won the other three.

The fixes are specific, and none of them is a talent problem.

🔧 Kelis Fisher, the sophomore guard from Baltimore, needs more minutes. Her athleticism changes the dynamic of this team.

🔧 Sarah Strong has to grow up a little on the defensive end. Ticky-tack fouls cost more when you have to play her this many minutes, and they've hurt UConn early this year. It's the same thing Geno means by "stupid fouls."

🔧 The young guards need reps. Blanca Quiñonez and Jovana Popovic get better one way: playing through it. With Arnold and Heckel out, there was nowhere to hide it Tuesday.

The schedule is the part I'd push on. UConn scheduled itself into a spot with no easy way out, and when Geno says maybe he did them a disservice, I hear a coach who knows the answer is to play through it, not to pull back. Get deeper into the Big East schedule and on the other side of the new year, and I expect them to be fine.

This is not one of his most talented UConn teams, but it's very good, and it has key pieces. They've lost a few hard games. You can't play Michigan and Louisville and not play well. I just wouldn't want to see this team in my bracket in March.

Your call: is this a coach motivating a young team, or a young team telling him something real? 👇

The Game · Washington

Eighty at the Half. Tatum Ends Boston's Skid.

Jayson Tatum scored 28 of his 41 before halftime, Boston led by 31 at the break, and the Celtics held on in Washington to snap a four-game losing streak and stay unbeaten in NBA Cup play.

By Westview Staff

41 PTS13-25 FG6-15 3PT9-14 FT28 1H PTS8 AST6 REB2 BLK37 MIN

Boston walked into Washington on a four-game skid, playing its third game in four nights. It scored 80 in the first half, tied for the third-highest halftime total in the league this season, per the Celtics' game notes. The Celtics led 34-28 after one, won the second quarter 46-21 and went to the locker room up 80-49. That was the game.

Tatum drove it. He went 13-of-25 for 41, with 15 points in the first quarter and 28 before the break. It was his 31st career 40-point game and his highest-scoring night since he returned from the Achilles injury. The read: 7-of-10 on twos, 14 points in the paint, and the three-ball (6-of-15) was a bonus. He also got to the line 14 times and made 9. That's the one number to clean up.

The system carried the rest. Boston made 22 of 51 threes (43.1%), six of them from Paul George, who finished with 28 points and 9 assists and became the eighth player in league history with 2,500 career threes. The Celtics assisted on 30 of their 46 makes and turned it over 10 times. Neemias Queta went 9-of-11 for a season-high 21, 18 of those in the paint.

Washington didn't fold. Anthony Davis went for 33 and 13, Kyshawn George added 24, and AJ Dybantsa, the No. 1 pick in June, had 20 points, 11 rebounds and 7 assists against his hometown Celtics. Dybantsa's bank shot made it 117-112. Then Baylor Scheierman, scoreless for the first 45½ minutes, scored eight straight Boston points in about 75 seconds, including a 31-footer that made it 127-114.

Washington (7-12) is now mathematically eliminated from the Cup. Boston (12-6) hosts Atlanta on Friday, and the winner finishes atop the group.

Scout's Note · Where it was decided

Boston made 22 threes to Washington's 12, a 30-point swing from one line (66 to 36). It also scored 23 points off 14 Wizards turnovers and gave up 15 off its own 10. Washington won the second half 67-51 and still lost by 15. A 31-point hole is a bad place to start learning how to guard.

NBA Cup · Orlando

Detroit Runs 18-0 and Wins in Orlando Without Cade

Pistons 104, Magic 86 · DET 11-6

Cade Cunningham was a late scratch, and Jalen Duren and Duncan Robinson sat too. Orlando led 27-20 in the second quarter. Detroit answered with an 18-0 run, won the quarter 32-14 and went to the half up 48-36. Isaiah Joe, in his first start for the Pistons, scored 21 on 8-of-15, 5-of-11 from three. Daniss Jenkins ran the group: 15 points, all in the first half, and 10 assists.

Ausar Thompson scored six, but read the rest of the line: 10 rebounds, 7 assists, 4 blocks, 3-of-3 from the floor, and Franz Wagner held to two first-half points. Orlando turned it over 20 times and got 9 fast-break points to Detroit's 19. Desmond Bane had 21 and Wagner 20. It was the Magic's first Cup loss, so they can't lock up East Group A yet.

NBA Cup · Denver

Flagg Ties His Career High With 12 Dimes. Dallas Runs Denver Off the Floor.

Mavericks 120, Nuggets 101 · DAL 11-7

Cooper Flagg went for 24 points, 6 rebounds and 12 assists with one turnover. Dallas closed the second quarter on a 15-0 run, led 58-39 at the half and was up as many as 22. The edge is the one every coach circles: the Mavs ran. They scored 35 fast-break points to Denver's 7, shot 15-of-21 in transition, and finished with 68 points in the paint to Denver's 38.

Caleb Martin had five of Dallas's 13 steals, and the Mavs turned 18 Denver turnovers into 26 points. Seven Mavericks scored in double figures, and the bench outscored Denver's 47-30. Jamal Murray had 20 and Nikola Jokic 18 and 11 for the Nuggets, who fell to 10-6. Dallas won its fourth straight and is 3-0 in West Group A.

NCAAM · LSU

The SEC Scheduled a Vote on LSU's Membership. A Roster Decision Started It.

The SEC said in a court filing Monday that its presidents and chancellors will meet Dec. 3, two days before the SEC Championship Game, to consider ending LSU's membership. Expulsion takes a two-thirds vote of qualifying members. CBS Sports reports it is considered unlikely, though frustration among league leaders is high.

The dispute centers on men's basketball and RJ Luis Jr., the former St. John's forward who signed two-way contracts with Utah and Boston in 2025, played three preseason games and never appeared in an NBA regular-season game. He committed to LSU in May and won a restraining order in August. A Louisiana judge has since issued a preliminary injunction that protects LSU from punishment for rostering him, and the SEC says in its filing it has "no avenue to penalize" LSU because of it. The league's penalty structure, approved this summer, calls for a half-season coach suspension and a fine of up to 50% of a sport's annual budget. LSU played Luis against Louisiana-Monroe on Nov. 18. On Nov. 19, LSU president Wade Rousse said the Tigers would no longer play him.

CBS Sports Yahoo Sports

My Take

A conference is voting on whether to remove a member that joined in 1933, and the trigger is a rotation decision about one player. That is not a story about LSU. It's a story about a sport with no written national standard for former pros and two-way signees. What it has instead is a patchwork: a league policy in one place, a restraining order in another, an injunction in a third.

The fix is boring and overdue. Write one eligibility standard. Apply it the same way at every school. Give it a review process that doesn't run through a county courthouse. Until then, a coach's rotation is a legal strategy. The filings are allegations until a court says otherwise, and LSU's side isn't in the documents we reviewed.
