The Game · WNBA Finals Game 7 · San Francisco

Two at the Half. Twenty-Eight After. Valkyries Win It All in Year Two.

Gabby Williams went to the locker room with two points in the first Game 7 in WNBA Finals history. She came out and authored the biggest half in league postseason history. Golden State 68, Atlanta 63.

By The Westview Group · Contributor: Keil Moore

30 PTS28 2ND-HALF PTS9-19 FG (2H)4-6 3PT (2H)6-6 FT (2H)4 BLK2 STLFINALS MVP

Atlanta led 30-24 at halftime on Halloween night at Chase Center. The team leading at the half had won all six previous games in this series. Then Gabby Williams took the game over. Fifteen in the third quarter, including Golden State's final 11 points of the frame. Twenty-eight in the half, the most in any half in WNBA postseason history, per CBS Sports. Her 30 tied Penny Taylor for the most points in a winner-take-all game.

It came down to the last 2:41. Rhyne Howard's layup tied it at 57. Williams answered with a three, then two free throws with 1:40 left. Allisha Gray, playing through an injured hand, hit two at the line with 11 seconds left to make it a one-possession game. Then a jump ball between Gray and Williams, Veronica Burton came up with it, and Burton iced it at the line with 5.1 seconds to go.

Context matters here. Golden State is in its second season. It is the first expansion team to win a title in Year 2, and per CBS Sports the first champion in WNBA history without an All-WNBA player on the roster. Natalie Nakase changed her starting five mid-series, moving Kaila Charles and Tiffany Hayes in and bringing Kayla Thornton and Cecilia Zandalasini off the bench. Hayes gave her 11 in Game 7. Kiah Stokes, now a four-time champion, pulled 13 rebounds with 2 blocks.

Atlanta forced Game 7 with an 80-69 win in Game 6 on Thursday behind Gray's 23 and Howard's 21. On Saturday, Howard went 3-of-14 for 6. Jordin Canada led the Dream with 18; Angel Reese had 8 and 12 with four turnovers.

Scout's Note · The second-half read

Williams didn't force it early. Two points at the break is a player reading coverage, not a player out of rhythm. After halftime she got her catches on the move, went 4-of-6 from three and 6-of-6 at the line, and defended the whole time: 4 blocks, 2 steals. That's a two-way wing deciding the game on both ends. Atlanta's backcourt went cold at the worst time: Howard 3-of-14. Golden State held the Dream to 63 and defended its way to a title.

San Antonio · Saturday

Wemby 43 and 6 Blocks. That's a Problem.

Spurs 121, Timberwolves 111

Victor Wembanyama went 17-of-34 for 43 points with 7 rebounds, 6 blocks and 4 assists against a Minnesota team missing Edwards and Gobert. Per AP, it's his 11th career 40-point game. Dylan Harper added 25 and 6, De'Aaron Fox 20, and San Antonio has won four straight.

Ayo Dosunmu gave the Wolves 27 and 9. LaMelo Ball played 20 minutes in foul trouble and fouled out with 3:43 left.

Los Angeles · Sunday

Luka: 45, 15 and 13-for-13 at the Line

Lakers 131, Raptors 125

Luka Dončić went 12-of-23, 8-of-12 from three and perfect on 13 free throws, with 15 rebounds, 6 assists and 5 steals in 35 minutes. The one blemish: 7 turnovers. Jamison Battle added 20 and Austin Reaves 19. Scottie Barnes had 29 for Toronto, RJ Barrett 22.

Bounce-back night. On Friday, Luka scored 21 in the first quarter, the Lakers led 38-15, and Golden State still won by 17.

San Francisco · Sunday

Rookie Lendeborg Drops 37 With Steph and Draymond Resting

Warriors 124, Clippers 112

Yaxel Lendeborg went 13-of-19, 6-of-11 from deep, for a career-high 37. It's the most by a Warriors rookie since Stephen Curry's 42 in April 2010. He joins Wilt Chamberlain and Rick Barry as the only Warriors with two 30-point games in their first seven. Golden State has won four straight after an 0-3 start; the Clippers have lost five in a row.

The Business

Front offices · contracts · the money side of the game

WNBA · Golden State

The Valkyries Promised a Title in Five Years. They Needed Two.

Joe Lacob promised a championship within five years when the Bay Area got its expansion team. Per AP, Golden State has sold out every home game at Chase Center since day one, made the playoffs in Year 1 and won it all in Year 2. Lacob credited team president Jess Smith, GM Ohemaa Nyanin and coach Natalie Nakase. The parade is Wednesday in San Francisco.

Rookie-Scale Options Get Picked Up

Washington exercised 2027-28 options on five players: Alex Sarr ($15.6M), Tre Johnson ($9.1M), Bub Carrington ($7.3M), Kyshawn George ($5.4M) and Will Riley ($3.9M). Charlotte picked up Kon Knueppel and Liam McNeeley; Atlanta picked up Asa Newell. Charlotte has until Monday on Tidjane Salaun's $10.4M fourth-year option.

My Take

Golden State won a title without an All-WNBA player. Read that twice, front offices. They didn't wait on a superstar. They built a defensive identity, a deep rotation and a coach willing to change her starting five in the middle of the Finals.

Every expansion team, WNBA or NBA, gets told to be patient and collect lottery tickets. The Valkyries showed another path: hire the right people, sell the building out, coach to an identity and stack competent players. That's a model, not a miracle. The teams that keep losing for five years should have to explain why it can't be copied.

Reform Desk

The Format Worked. Now Schedule It Like It Matters.

The best-of-seven Finals delivered the league's first Game 7, a sellout on Halloween night. Two days earlier, Game 6 tipped at 12:30 p.m. on a Thursday because of arena availability in Atlanta. A league that just produced its best night can't keep scheduling its biggest games around open dates. Lock the Finals buildings and windows before the playoffs start.
