The Game · NBA Cup

LeBron, 41, Dusts Off the Cape. Miami’s Streak Is Over.

At 41 years and 330 days, James broke his own record as the oldest player to post a triple-double, and Philadelphia needed all of it.

By Westview Staff

25 PTS10 REB12 AST9-15 FG4-6 3PT3-3 FT3 TO+14

His first triple-double in a 76ers uniform, and the 126th of his career. The 76ers beat the Heat 111-104, ended Miami’s seven-game win streak, and did it without Joel Embiid, then lost Jaylen Brown to his left hamstring before halftime.

The game turned in the second quarter. Miami won the first 32-29. James scored 14 straight points in the second, and Philadelphia took the period 33-21. Tyrese Maxey put up 30 on 12-of-21 with 6 assists. VJ Edgecombe added 18, 6 boards and 5 assists.

Miami got its stars’ numbers. Giannis Antetokounmpo had 32 and 14 on 11-of-18. Davion Mitchell had 20 and 10 assists. Bam Adebayo had 17 and 11. It wasn’t enough.

“If they need me to dust off the cape from time to time,” James said, “I can still accomplish that.” Erik Spoelstra: “Father Time always wins. But right now, he’s giving Father Time hell.” James turns 42 on Dec. 30.

Scout’s Note · Paint vs. arc

Miami scored 60 in the paint and still lost by seven. The Heat went 8-of-31 from three to Philadelphia’s 14-of-35, an 18-point gap, and left 8 points at the line (18-of-26). James’ 4-of-6 from deep is the tendency to track. When that shot falls, you can’t sit in the gap and wait on the drive, and 12 assists tell you what happens when help comes. The Sixers finished with 29.

NBA Cup · West B

McDaniels Takes Over. Then the Wolves Hold Their Breath.

Timberwolves 113, Thunder 106

Jaden McDaniels went for 32, 10 and 5 on 11-of-15, with 4-of-5 from three and 6-of-6 at the line. His 26-footer at the third-quarter horn capped a 24-10 run and put Minnesota up 87-83.

Oklahoma City had flipped this game with a 37-21 second quarter and led 73-63 later. The Wolves answered, made 17 threes to OKC’s 11, and survived a late push to 105-104. Minnesota is 3-0 in the Cup. The Thunder are out, at 10-9 with six losses in their last nine. The cost: LaMelo Ball’s left ankle.

NBA Cup · West C

Wemby Gets 44. Portland Wins the Math.

Trail Blazers 116, Spurs 111

Victor Wembanyama was 17-of-30 for 44 points and 16 boards. Deni Avdija answered with 33, 12 and 7, including 5-of-8 from three. Damian Lillard added 29 and went 10-for-10 at the line.

Portland shot 39.4% from the floor and still won. The Blazers went 19-of-50 from deep to San Antonio’s 10-of-36, turned it over only 8 times to the Spurs’ 14, and took the fourth 32-24. That’s shot volume and possession care beating a unicorn.

The Knicks Win the Fourth by 22

Knicks 121, Cavaliers 102

Cleveland led by 11 late in the third. Then New York won the fourth 39-17. Jose Alvarado scored 16 of his 21 in that quarter and finished 5-of-7 from three.

The film note is the bench and the ball movement: 45 bench points, 33 assists, 17-of-37 from three. Karl-Anthony Towns had 12, 10 and 7 assists for his 15th straight double-double. Donovan Mitchell’s 30 and James Harden’s 28 weren’t enough with Cleveland at 10-of-38 from deep. New York has won six straight.

NCAAM · LSU

Federal Judge Lets the SEC’s LSU Meeting Go Forward

On Wednesday a federal judge denied the SEC’s request for a temporary restraining order to stop LSU from legally interfering with a potential expulsion vote. The Dec. 3 presidents’ meeting on LSU’s membership still proceeds. The SEC says LSU assured the court it won’t interfere.

The fight centers on RJ Luis Jr., who signed with the Utah Jazz and played three preseason games for Boston in 2025 without appearing in a regular-season NBA game. LSU added him after a Louisiana judge’s preliminary injunction, and he debuted Nov. 18 against UL Monroe. On Nov. 19, LSU president Wade Rousse told coach Will Wade not to play him. LSU moved to dismiss on Tuesday. Louisiana Attorney General Liz Murrill has vowed to sue if the school is expelled.

NCAAM · NCAAW · Capitol Hill

Protect College Sports Act Eyes the House Floor

Roll Call reported Monday that a revised House version could reach the floor the week after Thanksgiving, with recess set for Dec. 17. The Senate passed its version in September. The House text would raise the conference cap from 20 to 22 and count school-linked NIL deals against the revenue-share cap.

My Take

A conference weighing expulsion over one roster spot is what happens when eligibility gets written one injunction at a time. AP’s count, as of Nov. 3: roughly 200 eligibility lawsuits and about 850 plaintiffs across all sports. Among basketball players, 171 men and 30 women had won some relief.

That is no way to run a roster. Coaches can’t build a game plan on a docket. Players can’t plan a career on one. We need one written standard for athletes with pro contracts and pro-game experience, applied the same way in every state, with a fast appeal and a hard deadline before opening night. Whether Congress, the NCAA or a negotiated agreement writes it, write it.

Durant Joins the 33,000 Club

A three with 46.3 seconds left in the second quarter made Kevin Durant the fifth player to score 33,000 points. The others: LeBron James (43,672), Kareem Abdul-Jabbar (38,387), Karl Malone (36,298) and Kobe Bryant (33,643). James made his own history the same night. Durant finished with 20 and 10.

Reform Desk

The Cup Has Made Margin a Stake. Say So Loudly.

Per Yahoo, the Knicks, 1-2 in group play, need to beat Miami by at least 7 on Friday and also need Indiana to beat Philadelphia. Point differential gives a late possession real weight, and fans and coaches should not need a calculator to see it. Put the live advancement math on every broadcast score bug. And keep asking whether raw margin is the right tiebreaker when it rewards running it up. A capped differential is worth a serious look.
