The Game · NCAAM · Las Vegas

Up 24. Whistled for a Timeout. Wooley Wins It Anyway.

No. 25 Louisville nearly gave one away to Rick Pitino's No. 13 St. John's. Then Adrian Wooley hit a corner three at the buzzer: 93-90.

By Westview Staff

TRIMBLE: 33 PTS12-19 FG1-3 3PT8-11 FT5 AST4 REB0 TO31 MIN

Louisville led 57-39 at the half and 68-44 with about 15 minutes left in this Players Era 16 semifinal. St. John's had gone 0-for-10 from three in the first half. Then the Red Storm went 5-of-9 after the break, used an 18-3 run to get within two and turned a blowout into a possession game.

With about 13 seconds left, Jackson Shelstad was trapped and officials granted a timeout Louisville didn't have. That's a technical. Ian Jackson made both free throws to tie it at 90. WDRB reported that the replay didn't appear to show Shelstad asking for one. Shelstad's account to ESPN, as relayed by WDRB: "I promise you, I did not call a timeout."

The last possession is the film clip. Seth Trimble drove, got caught, kept his poise and found Wooley in the corner with under two seconds left. Wooley buried it at the horn. "I had to make it. I just had to," he said.

Trimble was the engine all night: a career-high 33 off the bench with no turnovers. Wooley finished with 19 on 4-of-8 from three. Karter Knox added 15, and Flory Bidunga had 12 and nine boards. Ian Jackson led St. John's with 18, Keyshawn Hall had 17 and 10, and Quinn Ellis had 16 with six assists.

It was Pitino's first game against Louisville since the school fired him nine years ago. St. John's took its first loss and is 6-1. His read: "What I saw tonight was much more rewarding than 40-point victories early in the year." Louisville, 5-1, has now beaten ranked opponents on back-to-back nights. It gets No. 9 Tennessee tonight in the bracket final.

Scout's Note · The margin was shot volume

St. John's shot 51.8% and made eight more free throws (27 to 19). It still lost. Louisville took 10 more shots (66 to 56), won second-chance points 18-8 and went 13-of-14 at the rim. The warning for Louisville: from 68-44, St. John's outscored the Cards 46-25. Pressure offense and late-clock ball security are the next two film sessions.

NCAAW · Battle 4 Atlantis

Hidalgo Owns the Notre Dame Record Book's Top Line

Hannah Hidalgo's first-quarter three moved her past Arike Ogunbowale's program record of 2,626 points. She got there in 109 games; Ogunbowale's total took 149. The night's line: 22 points, nine steals, six rebounds and four assists in a 78-64 win over West Virginia. By our math she sits at 2,648.

The tape starts on defense. Notre Dame had 18 steals, forced 24 turnovers and turned them into 28 points. Hidalgo had 15 points off turnovers. Jacy Abii added 16 and 12.

Saint Mary's Shoots No. 6 UCLA Out of the Gym

NCAAM · LSU

RJ Luis Jr. Takes Sankey to Court, Personally

In an amended suit filed Wednesday in East Baton Rouge Parish, Luis seeks damages from the SEC and Commissioner Greg Sankey, including Sankey personally. The suit alleges Sankey threatened LSU's membership if Luis kept playing. It also alleges athletes at 14 of the SEC's 16 schools have competed under court orders this season. Those are allegations. No dollar figure is listed. SEC presidents and chancellors are set to meet Dec. 3 on LSU's membership. A contempt-motion hearing is set for Jan. 11.

An NBA Cup Title Is Worth $604,985 a Player

Runners-up get $241,994, semifinal losers $120,997 and quarterfinal losers $60,499. Two-way champions get $302,493. The winner's share is up $104,985, or 21%, from $500,000 in 2023. My Take

Once a commissioner is a defendant in his personal capacity, eligibility has moved from the compliance office to the courtroom. Nobody wins that way. Players can't plan, coaches can't build a rotation and conferences end up governing by threat and injunction. The fix is structural: one written standard, a fast neutral hearing and a decision that holds before the season tips. Decide it in October, not on a Thanksgiving-week docket.

The Culture

The reunion and the whistle

Reform Desk · The disputed timeout

One whistle handed St. John's two free throws and a tie with about 13 seconds left. The player involved says he never asked for time. WDRB reported the replay didn't appear to show that he did. Wooley's shot kept this from deciding the game. Officiating policy shouldn't depend on a buzzer-beater to bail it out.

My proposal: in the final two minutes, any technical for an excess timeout gets an automatic, quick look at who requested it. If the video and audio can't show a request, no free throws. Check it once, get it right, play on.
