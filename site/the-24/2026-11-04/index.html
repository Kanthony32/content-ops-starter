The Game · NCAAM · Lexington, Ky.

Momcilovic Hits Six, Wilkins Takes Seven. Kentucky Opens Loud.

With the NBA dark for Election Day, Rupp Arena had the stage. No. 23 Kentucky answered an early 13-9 deficit with a 25-3 run and two debut records.

By The Westview Group · Contributor: Keil Moore

MOMCILOVIC24 PTS8-12 FG6-9 3PT5 REBUK 85, MANH 57

Milan Momcilovic didn't need a feeling-out process. He went 8-of-12 from the floor and 6-of-9 from three for 24 points and 5 rebounds in his Kentucky debut. The six threes tied the UK debut record Antonio Reeves set in 2022.

The other record came on the other end. Alex Wilkins had 11 points, 5 assists and 7 steals, the most ever by a Kentucky player in his debut. That's the number that should worry SEC scouts. A guard who reads passing lanes like that turns a half-court game into a track meet.

Manhattan led 13-9 with 12:24 left in the first half. Then Kentucky went on a 25-3 run over six minutes, led 48-22 at the break, and pushed the margin as high as 83-44. Zoom Diallo added 12. Novak Manojlovic and Kevin Kearney scored 11 each for the Jaspers.

One caution flag: Mark Pope called guard Kam Williams "day-to-day" before tip and said Kentucky will "require every light to be green" before he plays. Depth gets tested in November, not March.

Scout's Note · The pressure league

Tuesday's ranked openers had one theme: turn people over and run. Kentucky's run came off Wilkins' hands. Arkansas opened on a 25-0 run and forced 24 turnovers. Virginia had 18 steals and a 29-7 edge in fast-break points. Alabama scored 33 points off 23 Sam Houston turnovers. Guard play against mid-majors flatters everybody, so watch what happens when these teams see a guard who can handle a trap.

NCAAM · NCAAW · what moved Tuesday

NCAAM · Charlottesville, Va.

De Ridder Plays Like the ACC Player of the Year

No. 9 Virginia 89, Drexel 60

The preseason ACC Player of the Year scored 12 of Virginia's first 14 points, starting with a layup five seconds in and two straight threes. Thijs De Ridder finished with 24 and 9 on 9-of-15. Sam Lewis added 20 on 4-of-6 from deep, and Johann Grunloh had 11 and 9.

The defense did the rest. Virginia had 18 steals, scored 25 points off 21 Drexel turnovers and won fast-break points 29-7. Ryan Odom's group won 30 games last year. It looks like it wants more.

NCAAM · Knoxville, Tenn.

Tennessee Was Tied at the Half. Then 28-7.

No. 8 Tennessee 90, Wofford 59

Wofford hung around, 34-31 at halftime, and won the first-half glass 21-19. Then Tennessee opened the second half on a 28-7 run and it was over. DeWayne Brown II put up 17 points and 17 rebounds, 12 of the points after the break. Terrence Hill Jr. added 17 and Tyler Lundblade 12 on 4-of-9 from three.

Tennessee shot 34-of-65 (52.3%). Rick Barnes is in year 12, and this is what his teams do: make the second half a physical problem.

NCAAW · Durham, N.C.

Duke Shuts the Door After Halftime

No. 4 Duke 76, Green Bay 60

It was 33-31 at the half. Green Bay then shot 6-of-27 (22.2%) after halftime. Arianna Roberson had 15 points and 4 blocks, Riley Nelson scored 15, and Texas transfer Aaliyah Crump went 11-5-6 in her Duke debut.

Duke won the glass 44-31 with 19 second-chance points, and did it without Toby Fournier. Maddy Skorupski led Green Bay with 16 and 6.

The Business

Scheduling · the league office · the money side of the game

NBA · League Office

The NBA Took Election Day Off. Wednesday Pays for It.

None of the NBA's 30 teams played Tuesday. Citing the league's release, Yahoo Sports reported the league said the off night "will encourage fans and the broader NBA community to make a plan to vote and to participate in the civic process." The NHL played a full schedule. So did college basketball.

The bill comes due tonight: 14 games involving 28 teams, led by 76ers-Pistons at 7 and Mavericks-Spurs at 9:30 ET. The league has other planned dark dates this season, too: Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, the All-Star break and April 10.

Yahoo Sports Wednesday slate

My Take

Giving up a Tuesday of inventory is a real cost, and the NBA paid it on purpose. That's the right call. A league that asks its players to be civic voices should give the whole building, from players to arena workers, a night to vote.

Here's the consistency test. If one night off is worth it for the civic process, the schedule should be built around it every cycle, not announced as a gesture. Put it in the scheduling rules and stop making it a press release.
