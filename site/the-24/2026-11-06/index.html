NCAAW · Ann Arbor, Mich.

Michigan Ends UConn's 39-Game Run on Opening Night

No. 5 Michigan beat No. 2 UConn 77-66 at Crisler Center. The Huskies hadn't lost a regular-season game since Feb. 6, 2025. Kim Barnes Arico's group didn't just survive the moment. It controlled it.

By The Westview Group · Contributor: Keil Moore

DELFOSSE22 PTS (career high)8 FG4 3PT9 REBMICH 77 · UCONN 66

Season opener. Top-five matchup. The program that owns the regular season walks into your building. Michigan treated it like a home game it expected to win, and that's the headline.

Te'Yala Delfosse set the tone: a career-high 22 points on eight field goals, four of them threes, plus nine rebounds. Olivia Olson added 19. Courtney Ogden had 12 and Syla Swords hit three threes for 10. Four players in double figures against a defense that usually takes away your first and second options.

It was the first top-five women's game ever played at Crisler, and only Michigan's third win over a top-five team, per the school. It's the end of a UConn streak of 39 straight regular-season wins.

UConn's side of the ledger: Jovana Popovic scored 25 and hit a jumper at the third-quarter buzzer. But Sarah Strong picked up three fouls, sat the entire second quarter, and eventually fouled out with 13 points in 27 minutes. Take your best player off the floor for a quarter on the road against a top-five team, and you're asking a lot of everyone else.

Scout's Note · Foul trouble is a choice

Geno Auriemma has always said foul trouble is an indication of immaturity. There was a time I thought that was a harsh critique. Now I couldn't agree more. Getting in a stance, being prepared to defend, understanding tendencies: all of that keeps Sarah Strong on the floor. Instead she spent the second quarter on the bench, and not with two fouls but with three. Ultimately the aggression that makes Strong who she is was gone for the rest of the game.

With Strong on the bench for all of the second, UConn lost its rim protection, its help-side length and the post touches it plays through. Michigan's answer was spacing: Delfosse, Swords and Olson combined for eight threes. Popovic's 25 kept UConn in it, but one scorer can't cover for a missing anchor on the road.

Next for UConn: No. 10 Iowa in Storrs on Sunday. Let's see how the preseason All-American responds.

NBA · San Francisco

Steph Passes the Big O. Warriors Win Six Straight.

Warriors 120, Heat 100

A first-quarter three moved Stephen Curry past Oscar Robertson (26,710) into 17th on the NBA's career scoring list. He finished with 29 and six threes, after a 2-of-10 first half. That's the Curry tendency in one line: the miss doesn't change the next shot.

Golden State broke it open with a 26-9 run in the third and has won six straight since an 0-3 start. Draymond Green handed out a season-high 11 assists; rookie Yaxel Lendeborg went for 17, 8 and 6. For Miami, Davion Mitchell scored 22 and Giannis had 20 and 14. The Heat are 3-6.

NBA · Phoenix

Booker's 23-Point Half Snaps Phoenix's Skid

Suns 117, Magic 104

One night after losing at home to Portland, Devin Booker had 23 by halftime on 8-of-13 and finished with 30 on 11-of-22, with 8 assists and 3 steals. That's 30-plus in back-to-back games and 89 points over his last three. Dillon Brooks added 24 and Miles Bridges a season-high 13 boards with 18 points.

Phoenix went 17-of-34 from three (50%) and turned 25 Orlando turnovers into 36 points. Wendell Carter Jr. was terrific in the loss, 27 and 13 on 10-of-12. Franz Wagner: 15 points, 15 assists. The Magic, without Banchero and Suggs, have dropped three straight.

NCAAM · NCAAW · Washington

The Clock Restarts on the College Sports Bill

The Protect College Sports Act passed the Senate 77-22 in October. The House lame-duck session opens Nov. 9, and it has to decide whether to take the bill up after the election. The players' group NCPA says roughly 70% of Division I departments fall under the bill's $50 million revenue threshold for protecting Olympic and women's sports, a figure the USOPC doesn't dispute, per AP.

The NCPA's Ramogi Huma: "My hope is the election tanks the bill, because there's just not enough time." The NCAA's Tim Buckley argued that not acting "would mean not enshrining these transformative student-athlete benefits into federal law."

AP via News4JAX

NCAAM · St. Louis

Avila Wins His Injunction. Clemson's Hunter Files.

A St. Louis judge granted Saint Louis forward Robbie Avila, the A-10 player of the year, a preliminary injunction to play a fifth season, with SLU allowed a 16th roster spot until trial. The NCAA could appeal. The same day, Clemson guard Dillon Hunter sued the NCAA and Clemson in Pickens County, S.C. He's out tonight against American unless he gets a ruling first. Avila and the Billikens play Missouri tonight at 7.

AP via WSLS Hunter suit

NBA · Ownership

Vegas Down to Two Groups, Per The Ringer

The Ringer reports Las Vegas expansion is down to two bidders, the Laurie group (now with Silver Lake) and Bill Foley's group with Jerry Colangelo, with a decision expected by year's end. It also reports a Dec. 8 hearing in the Buss family dispute that could affect Jeanie Buss' governor status.

The Ringer

My Take

Look at the two stories side by side. Congress is trying to write athlete rules into federal law in a lame-duck window. And on the same day, a county judge in Missouri decided who plays for Saint Louis this season, while another one in South Carolina got asked to decide the same thing for Clemson.

That's the system right now: eligibility decided case by case, courthouse by courthouse, a week into the season. Whether this bill is the answer or not, the sport needs one standard, written down, negotiated with the people it governs, and applied the same way everywhere. Rushing it through in a few weeks isn't that. Neither is the status quo.

Reform Desk

131-22 Isn't a Game. It's an Invoice.

LSU won by 109. Duke won by 61. Nobody learned anything. Texas Southern shot 8-of-55 in a game that was a mismatch the day it was signed. That's not on the players. It's on a scheduling model that pays HBCU and low-major programs to absorb blowouts. If the sport wants November to matter, give those schools real money and real competition, like more neutral-site events and shared revenue from multi-team events, instead of a check for taking a beating.
