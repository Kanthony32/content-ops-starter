The Game · Cleveland

Ant Drops 47. Cleveland Wins Anyway.

Edwards went 14-of-18 inside the arc and scored 11 in overtime on a knee he was questionable with. The Cavs answered with the thing that wins in October and in May: everybody else.

By The Westview Group · Contributor: Keil Moore

47 PTS19-34 FG14-18 2PT5-15 3PT4-6 FT11 OT PTS41 MIN

Anthony Edwards was listed questionable with right knee management, on the second night of a back-to-back. He played 41 minutes and put up 47. It's the seventh time he's scored 47 or more, and his best night ever against Cleveland, per the AP. He now owns two of the four biggest scoring nights in the league this season. Luka's 52 against Utah is still on top.

The scouting read is simple. Cleveland could not stay attached to him coming off pin-downs, and once he got downhill he lived at the rim: 14-of-18 on twos, 9-of-10 in the paint. The jumper came and went at 5-of-15 from three. The paint never closed.

Minnesota still lost, 127-125, because basketball is a five-man sport. Donovan Mitchell scored 26, including the 19-foot fadeaway with 48 seconds left in overtime that put Cleveland ahead for good. James Harden added 19 and 9 assists and buried two free throws with 13 seconds left. Edwards got one more look, wide open at the top of the key, with a second overtime on the line. It didn't drop.

Do the math on the night. Ant's teammates scored 78. Mitchell's scored 101. And Meleek Thomas, the 34th pick in June, gave Cleveland 16 in his NBA debut on 4-of-5 from three. That's depth showing up on a Monday.

Scout's Note · Where it was lost

Minnesota went 14-of-23 at the line (60.9%) and 1-of-4 in overtime. Cleveland went 5-of-5 in OT. Add 18 Wolves turnovers, seven of them from LaMelo Ball, and you've found your two points. Rudy Gobert pulled 20 rebounds and went 0-for-9 from the floor. You can't play 4-on-5 in crunch time against a team that guards like Cleveland.

WNBA Finals · Game 5

Golden State Is One Win From a Title in Year Two

Valkyries 64, Dream 55 · GSV leads 3-2

This was a defensive clinic. Kiah Stokes, a career 3.3-point scorer, went 5-of-6 for 13 points with 8 boards, 3 blocks and 3 threes, and couldn't finish her on-court interview over the MVP chants. Veronica Burton added 13 and 8 with four blocks, tying Diana Taurasi (2009) for the most by a guard in a Finals game.

Atlanta scored 22 in the first half, its lowest postseason half ever. Angel Reese still pulled 15 rebounds but was held to 7 points, 2 after halftime. Rhyne Howard led the Dream with 20. Golden State now has 37 blocks in this series, a Finals record.

Miami

Flagg Outduels Giannis, and Miami Is 0-4

Mavericks 113, Heat 109

Back from a shoulder sprain, Cooper Flagg went for 29 points, 11 assists, 5 steals and 3 blocks. He scored 12 in the fourth and rejected Andrew Wiggins' go-ahead three with 31 seconds left. That's a 19-year-old closing out a former MVP on his floor.

Giannis Antetokounmpo got his first triple-double in a Heat uniform, 29 points and 11 assists, and it didn't matter. Miami missed 11 of 25 free throws. PJ Washington added 20 and Kyrie Irving 18 for Dallas.

Denver

Rookie Lendeborg Goes for 31 and 12. Steph Doesn't Miss.

Warriors 134, Nuggets 114

Golden State hadn't started 0-4 since 2007-08 and wasn't about to now. No. 11 pick Yaxel Lendeborg out of Michigan put up 31, 12 and 6 and lived at the line, 12-of-13. Stephen Curry went 8-for-8 from the floor, 3-for-3 from deep and 5-for-5 at the stripe, with 21 of his 24 after halftime.

The Warriors hit 22 of 44 threes and won the glass 50-36. Aaron Gordon picked up two technicals and was gone before it got out of hand.

NCAAM · Auburn

Auburn Fires Its GM a Week Before the Opener

Auburn fired men's basketball general manager Brian Kloman on Monday. According to multiple reports, he knew Lithuanian guard Mantas Rubstavicius had taken college courses in Europe and didn't tell anyone, and that came out during Rubstavicius' eligibility lawsuit against the NCAA. The 24-year-old dropped the suit and is headed back to Europe. Kloman was hired in March from Louisville. Auburn opens Nov. 4 against Southeastern Louisiana.

The Auburn Plainsman Yahoo Sports

My Take

Everybody rushed to hire a GM because the NBA has them. The NBA also has a CBA, a cap and a league office that knows exactly what it's looking at. College basketball built the job title before it built the rulebook.

Until the eligibility standard for former pros and international players is written down and applied the same way at every school, programs will keep litigating rosters in October. That's a governance problem. Fix the standard and the GM job gets a lot simpler.
